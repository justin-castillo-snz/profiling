# Ireland

## One collection point; several statistical uses

The Central Statistics Office's LCU, established in its reported form in 2009 and a stand-alone division since 2024, handles the largest foreign-owned multinational groups. Rather than reconciling all returns after collection, it designs combined monthly, quarterly and annual forms, removes repeated questions, edits unit records and delivers a clean file to the relevant domains. Those domains continue compilation; the LCU does **not** compile national accounts. The quarterly return includes profit and loss and balance-sheet information. [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§6.2.1–6.2.3, 6.2.6; [collection mandates](../concepts/lcu-coordination.md).

**What accounts cannot settle alone.** Intangible assets on balance sheets and inventory holdings indicate who may own intellectual property and production output. The team asks the group who owns inputs and stocks during production, whether a shipment actually changes ownership, where the intellectual property is resident, and who pays royalties to whom. It can then check whether the resulting trade flows, capital stocks, depreciation and profits tell a consistent story in national accounts and the balance of payments. before and after reconciliation. A discrepancy in the aggregates sends the team back to these earlier enquiries. (§§6.2.4–6.2.5, pp. 42–43; [profile quality](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md).)

**2025 Eurostat overview.** Eurostat's paper mentions interactive monthly, quarterly and annual survey forms as the Irish example; its slides give the start year as 2009, consistent with the account above. ([Paper](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-paper-2025.md), pp. 8–10; [slides](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md), 7.)
