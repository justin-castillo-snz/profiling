# Italy

## Follow an event from first signal to verified structural change

Established in 2017, ISTAT's LCU draws part-time specialists from registers, business and trade statistics, and national accounts. It selects a top-group list annually using size and organisation, considers the European top-tier list, and asks domains which groups require national or international profiling. Daily merger and acquisition news is a lead, not a register update: staff verify the event and reference year, identify the units in the business register, and establish control links where new identifiers are needed. [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§6.7–6.7.1, pp. 62–64; [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md).

**A branch changed the interpretation of a sale.** A manufacturer's reported foreign turnover exceeded recorded exports. Checks against trade data, accounts, VAT and invoicing narrowed the possibilities but did not settle the counterparty's residence. Direct confirmation through the turnover survey team established that sales went to a foreign company's **permanent establishment in Italy**. They were domestic sales. The intervention led to revision of the turnover split, an updated group panel and profiling of the complex enterprise. It was not a reason to raise exports until the totals agreed. (§6.7, Annex 1, pp. 70–73; [group perimeter and residence](../units/group-and-enterprise.md), [profile change](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md).)

**Infrastructure versus evidence.** The 2024–2026 task force mapped domain variables and built indicators for discrepancies in flows, governance and branch identification. Common unit identifiers and source metadata allow analysts to trace differences. The text presents expansion and real-time monitoring as development priorities; it does not claim branch flows can always be recovered from balance sheets. (§§6.7.2–6.7.6.)

**2025 Eurostat overview.** Eurostat describes Italy's EWS activity as web monitoring of merger-and-acquisition news, a single access point for enterprise data submission and plans to extend consistency indicators to short-term statistics. The slides date the unit to 2017 while the paper says "2017–18" for the group of later adopters. ([Paper](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-paper-2025.md), pp. 5, 9–10; [slides](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md), 7.) This matches the guideline account above.

## Profiling-team arrangement reported in 2020

The [Eurostat 2020 manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §6.B.1, pp. 97–100, places ISTAT's profiling team in the business-register section of its Economic Statistics Directorate. It reports six FTE responsible for group structure, employment, turnover and classification; consolidation, economic data collection and analysis were handled in the SBS section. The same team also processed EGR data. In November 2018, a pilot SBS survey began to validate enterprise structures and collect enterprise-level data for about 70 large groups. The manual describes the LCU in the same directorate as a connection to national accounts and other domains; this is organizational proximity, not evidence that profiling and LCU work were identical. The account is a dated snapshot; see [LCU coordination](../concepts/lcu-coordination.md).
