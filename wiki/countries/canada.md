# Canada

## Statistics Canada's complex-business profile

[UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F2, pp. 361–367; Box 7.3; Annex C3. These describe the Canadian Statistical Business Register (CSBR) as reported in the Guidelines, not a verified current manual.

**Two structures, linked.** Profilers build a legal hierarchy from tax records and majority ownership, and an operating hierarchy from the places where labour and capital are used. A location links to its parent establishment; each establishment links to its controlling statistical company. The enterprise directs domestic operations; the company is the level at which income, expenditure and balance-sheet accounts are maintained. The levels serve different accounting needs (F2, pp. 361–363, figs F2.1–F2.3).

**Ask about accounts, not just addresses.** Determine each operating unit's industry, workforce and, where available, revenue. Can costs and revenue both be obtained there, or only employment and partial accounts? The answer helps determine whether the unit is a location, an establishment or both. Several locations can share one establishment's accounts. Responsibility-centre language lets a respondent describe the business in familiar terms and tells survey programmes what can actually be supplied. The annex cautions that this granularity is expensive unless programmes need it. See [the Canadian accounting test and the UN definitions](../units/activity-and-location.md#the-canadian-accounting-test) and [reporting units](../units/observation-and-reporting.md). (F2, pp. 361–363.)

**Prioritise and scope separately.** Establishments are assessed by industry, geography, revenue and employment. A critical establishment brings its enterprise into annual prioritisation; that is not a requirement for an annual full-group interview. For a specific case, inspect update requests, discrepancies and profile age. One legal unit may need only its parent and operations checked. Group-wide disruption calls for checking all related companies; the account recommends a full group profile when the last one is more than three years old. (F2, pp. 363–365; [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md).)

**Research before contact.** Annotate a working copy using tax registration and schedules, payroll, surveys, interviewer feedback, earlier decisions, gazettes and public filings. Check changes of control, consolidation, trading status, openings, closures, asset sales, and reporting capacity. Notify affected programmes of proposed statistical changes and let them challenge them. Contact the business only for remaining gaps; aim, where possible, to limit contact to once a year. Begin by phone with a marked-up structure and prioritised questions, directed to the person who understands the issue. (F2, pp. 364–366.)

**Validate before and after the live update.** Check allocated operating-unit revenue and jobs against tax totals up the hierarchy (*vertical coherence*); compare allocations with survey, programme and administrative figures (*horizontal coherence*). Check whether the combined units still support the enterprise's principal industry. Investigate discrepancies rather than manually replacing automated legal-entity financial fields. Apply updates, recheck and file the profile report. The register journal can preserve why a decision was made alongside a log of old/new values, sources and dates. Annex C3 describes the associated signal queue and structure manager; C3.6 adds a workload module that prioritises and assigns signals needing manual review to analysts or profilers, who implement or cancel the change request. (F2, pp. 365–367; Box 7.3; Annex C3.3–C3.6; [change and quality](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md).)
