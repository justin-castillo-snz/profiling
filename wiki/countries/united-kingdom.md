# United Kingdom

## A desk profile can still involve the business

[UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F8, pp. 391–392. Size initially identified cases; referrals from other teams and the number of activities influenced priority and assignment to a senior or junior profiler. Most were desk cases **with telephone contact**; visits were reserved for a small number of complex cases. The account describes voluntary participation and clearer reporting requirements as a benefit for the business. This is a description of the UK case, not a general statement of legal obligations.

**Use a standard template to expose mismatches.** Check the previous profile, survey comments and response history before contacting the business. Compare group employment with payroll, and ownership and subsidiaries in a commercial source with the Inter-Departmental Business Register. Check legal-unit trading status; the enterprise, [reporting and local-unit links](../units/observation-and-reporting.md); survey contacts; duplicate sites; industry descriptions; and turnover and employment against other survey and administrative sources. (F8, pp. 391–392.)

**Close the loop.** Take discrepancies to the business by phone or visit, act on the findings in the register, attach the report to survey management and the case log, and date completion. An interaction is not the endpoint: changed collection arrangements must reach the next survey. See [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md) and [profile change and quality](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md).

## National profiling organization reported in 2020

The [Eurostat 2020 manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §6.B.1, pp. 95–96 and Table 1, describes the ONS profiling team as part of the business-register department. Its narrative reports 16 FTE responsible for recording reporting structures, employment, turnover and classification for the top 2,500 UK groups; data collection, validation and analysis sat elsewhere. Table 1 instead lists 13.84 FTE, a discrepancy the manual does not explain. The manual credits register experience and coordination with EGR work as strengths, while identifying fragmented ownership of the full group picture and reactive triggers from enterprise survey changes as limitations. It reports no automatic process in the table. These are historical organizational observations, not current staffing information.

## Enterprise as a survey unit

The [2025 Wiesbaden presentation](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slide 8, includes the UK among places where the enterprise is a core business-survey unit. The slide does not list surveys or show that the enterprise supplies every return. This describes a [statistical or observation unit, not necessarily the reporting unit](../units/observation-and-reporting.md), and adds no steps to the UK's desk profiling case above.
