# Switzerland

## A recurring workplace-employment profile

[UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F7, pp. 389–391; Boxes 7.2, 7.3 and 7.6. The Federal Statistical Office's case includes large private businesses and public administrations. Its purpose was both to update structures and to put employment in the right place.

**Agree a sustainable delivery.** An initial meeting established what the organisation could extract from its personnel system and how future deliveries would work. Quarterly data could arrive as workplace aggregates or staff lists. The business's own workplace keys had to be mapped to register units; establishing and maintaining those links could be the most labour-intensive part. One reporting office could therefore supply many [local units](../units/activity-and-location.md), without jobs being attributed to head office. (F7, pp. 389–390; [reporting units](../units/observation-and-reporting.md).)

**Validate coverage, not just totals.** Format and check plausibility, compare the delivery to the group's register structure, update missing or changed units, compare employment with the previous quarter, and query unexplained fluctuations. The case also describes checks against social insurance totals, activity codes and regional employment, and sharing reports with statistical users. After validation, data feed the register and employment statistics. F7 reports roughly 30% of national employment covered and 9.2 full-time equivalents; these are case figures, not present-day estimates. (F7, pp. 390–391; [profile quality](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md).)

**Light profiling and the NOGA survey are different mechanisms.** Box 7.6 (pp. 176–177) says profiling rests on a close partnership with large multi-establishment enterprises that deliver quarterly information, which keeps company structures and activity codes current and considerably reduces burden on those enterprises. This is distinct from the NOGA quality survey for single-local-unit enterprises, a quarterly sample in which enterprises already stating their activity (for example in their address) or with a website, and those in other surveys, are removed; the remainder are in four size classes checked every two to five years (more than 100 employees every 2 years; 10–99 every 3; 3–9 every 4; 1–2 every 5; fig. 7.3). Box 7.2 says all enterprises with more than 10 local units or more than 100 employees are contacted directly every quarter for structure and employment; single-local-unit enterprises are maintained from administrative sources and survey feedback; and "all other enterprises" form the *light profiling* group, contacted annually by internet survey for structural information and employment. (Analyst inference: this residual group is probably multi-local-unit enterprises below the intensive thresholds.) It should not be assumed to match European light manual profiling. (Boxes 7.2, 7.6.)

**Threshold unresolved.** Box 7.2 describes the intensive population as enterprises with **more than 10 local units or more than 100 employees** and describes annual online light profiling for the intermediate group. F7's synopsis instead describes enterprise groups with **more than 100 employees and 10 local units**. The connective and the unit of reference differ. Neither text establishes which rule applies now. Box 7.6 separately describes a NOGA activity-code survey for single-local-unit enterprises; that survey is not intensive profiling. (Box 7.2, pp. 156–157; Box 7.6; F7, p. 390; [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md).)

## Profiling-team comparison reported in 2020

The [Eurostat 2020 manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), Chapter 6, Table 1, p. 99, reports a Swiss model covering about 32,000 groups, with the top 400 manually profiled quarterly by an eight-point-nine-FTE team in the business-register setting. It describes a register-maintenance approach and a goal of maintaining structure and collecting/analyzing employment. The table says an algorithm was expected to treat all groups in 2019; that is a plan reported at the time, not evidence that it was implemented or remains current. This national organizational comparison is distinct from the UN's workplace-employment case above.
