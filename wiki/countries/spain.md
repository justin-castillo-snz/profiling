# Spain

The [approaches catalogue](../analyses/profiling-approaches.md#top-down-from-the-group-to-autonomous-enterprises) compares INE's use of business lines with other group-first methods.

## Intensive profiling: test the proposed enterprise with the people who run it

Spain's INE established its LCU in 2022, reporting directly to the presidency. Its Profiling Area identifies group perimeters, enterprises and relationships and monitors restructurings. Its Globalisation Area studies how the resulting units' production arrangements are reflected in national accounts and the balance of payments. Intensive profiling began in early 2023; the portfolio reached 20 groups in 2024 and 41 in 2025. Ranking combines employment, turnover, subsidiaries and geographic scope with cases flagged by cross-source inconsistencies. [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §6.6, pp. 56–61; [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md).

- **Desk research:** Establish the [control perimeter](../units/group-and-enterprise.md) from consolidated and individual accounts, looking specifically for controlled entities omitted from consolidation. Then propose enterprises using operating segments or, where available, business lines. The second decision turns on autonomy, not shareholding.
- **Validate with the group:** A letter from the INE president opens contact with senior management. The director responsible for consolidated accounts is often best placed to test the provisional delineation; operations or tax staff can explain particular flows. If the map does not fit, continue by email or telephone rather than forcing a decision in the meeting. Explain which existing returns will be better understood and protect confidentiality. (§6.6, pp. 58–59; [engagement](../concepts/lcu-coordination.md).)
- **Use the profile:** Investigate processing, merchanting, factoryless production or intangible assets where physical movements and economic ownership differ. The Globalisation Group connects INE domains, the central bank and tax agency to discuss recording and revisions. The centralised automated review system was described as work in progress, not the method that by itself determines enterprises. (§6.6, pp. 59–61; [European coordination](../concepts/european-profiling.md).)

## INE's own account (Wiesbaden Group, 2025): two-stage selection, annual calendar and a proposed single reporting channel

The Eurostat account above is a third-party summary; the INE authors' [paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md) and [presentation](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-presentation-2025.md) (Johannesburg, October 2025) add the following. Section numbers refer to the paper. The authors assess their own programme, and the benefits are asserted, not independently measured.

- **Institution (§3.2).** The LCU was created by Ministerial Order ETD/378/2022 as a division reporting to the INE Presidency. It coordinates production units rather than replacing them. Its functions include the multiannual intensive profiling plan, direct engagement, monitoring reorganisations, microdata validation, globalisation analysis and representation in the European LCU network and Early Warning System. See [LCU coordination](../concepts/lcu-coordination.md).
- **Selection (§4.1).** A two-stage composite complexity index (employees, turnover, countries, subsidiaries): first on global activity from the EuroGroups Register for Spanish-controlled groups, then on Spain-only activity from the national register whatever the parent's nationality. Top groups are chosen within each of ten A10 activity categories, financial groups are excluded, and portfolio size is set by resources. This is a resource-capped, activity-balanced portfolio rule; see [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md#a-two-stage-complexity-index-spain-2025).
- **Portfolio (§4.2, §4.4).** 5 groups in 2022, 20 in the 2024 portfolio (reference year 2023) and 41 in the 2025 portfolio (reference year 2024, including foreign-headquartered groups). Coverage rose from 2.4% to 3.2% of employment; Spanish-headquartered groups alone cover 28.5% of employment and 37% of turnover. The turnover is generated in Spain and is not output or value added. The portfolio-year labels differ between the paper and the presentation (see the source notes).
- **Procedure (§4.3).** A presidential letter and a headquarters meeting; the financial consolidation team is the best contact. The control perimeter is fixed first (including units absent from registers), then statistical enterprises are delineated and consolidated. Results flow to the register and the EGR on an annual calendar, and the previous year's profiling feeds structural business statistics. Follow-up is an annual email about perimeter changes. See [profiling workflow](../concepts/profiling-workflow.md#intensive-profiling-and-annual-follow-up-spain-2025) and [group and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md#unit-vocabulary-ine-spain-2025).
- **Lessons (§5).** Direct contact improves quality; the cross-cutting role gives coherence; European networks are essential; integrating the LCU with traditional production lines is hard. Work with group's consolidation team and collect the info through a single coordinated channel
- **Proposal, not implemented (§6): MNEData.** A secure platform where a group uploads one predefined variable set for all resident legal units, replacing many separate questionnaires (the slides cite more than 2,000 a year for one group). Indicative roadmap: 2025 approval, 2026 pilot, 2027–28 first operational run. The authors cite similar centralised collection in Canada, the UK and Ireland ([Canada](canada.md), [Ireland](ireland.md), [United Kingdom](united-kingdom.md); not described in these sources beyond the mention).

## Analyst notes on the INE account (user notes, not a published source)

These are the wiki owner's reading notes on the [INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md) and [presentation](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-presentation-2025.md). They are analyst interpretation. Where they go beyond or differ from the documents, this is flagged.

- **Six key learnings (notes):** keep an up-to-date, cross-domain view of enterprise groups; expect difficulty integrating diverse data sources; recognise the limits of traditional collection; engage large businesses directly to improve accuracy; build trust and cooperation with respondents; tackle methodological and operational challenges together. These condense the paper's §5 lessons; the paper does not present them as a numbered list.
- **Operational challenges (paper §5, in the notes' words):** clear communication channels, continuity of the contact point, and capacity to handle ad hoc requests; follow-up versus onboarding new groups; multi-source integration (administrative registers, surveys, commercial databases, direct company information) needing IT tools for harmonisation, validation and reconciliation; and embedding the LCU in existing structures while gradually adapting them, which can meet resistance from experts reluctant to change workflows or share control across domains.
- **Methodological challenge, "surveying is dead" (notes):** this is the notes' shorthand. The paper's qualified claim is that survey-based collection is poorly suited to the largest groups, and it points to digital platforms and data exchange supported by direct contact. The documents do not say surveys should be abandoned.
- **Three-stage reading of intensive profiling (notes):** perimeter, then enterprise delineation, then statistical consolidation (ancillary and integrated units excluded from group output; activity destined abroad treated specially, marked "?" in the notes). The paper describes two phases, with consolidation inside the second. The notes' stage 3 is therefore an analyst split, not the paper's structure. The paper says the perimeter is set with the group, and that delineating enterprises needs direct interaction, which the authors call a main value added of intensive profiling.
- **Future mode (notes):** integrated, forward-looking collection from large businesses via centralised coordination, tailored exchange protocols and secure real-time platforms. The paper frames this as the MNEData proposal (not implemented); "real time" and "advanced analytics" are stronger than the paper's "near real time" and its analysis service.
- **Points to verify before reuse:**
  - *Growth "5 to 41 in two years":* the paper's labels give 2022 → 5 and a 2025 portfolio of 41 (reference year 2024); the slides label 41 as 2024. Two to three years depending on the label; see the source notes.
  - *"GEN":* the paper counts enterprise groups (GEG), not global enterprises (GEN).
  - *Follow-up "at the beginning of each year":* the paper says an annual email asks about perimeter changes; the start of the year is not stated in the text read.

## Eurostat's 2025 summary of the Spanish LCU

Eurostat's Wiesbaden paper describes Spain as a relationship-based direct-engagement model with a future vision of a centralised automated system for microdata linking and unstructured information, and the slides give ES 2022. This is consistent with INE's own account above and is secondary reporting. ([Paper](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-paper-2025.md), pp. 9–10; [slides](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md), 7.)
