# Netherlands

## Statistics Netherlands (CBS): structural profiling and consistency work

[UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F5, pp. 369–379. The case traces decisions from 2005 and the ConGO unit from 2010. The populations, staffing and proposed reforms are historical, not current targets.

**Select the work.** The historical Top-X comprised about 1,900 groups; roughly 300 were in ConGO (*Consistentie Grote Ondernemingen*) for additional cross-statistic checks. A 0–10 complexity and statistical impact score combined control layers, legal and fiscal units, size and contribution to an industry. Register staff and users could nominate exceptions, including international complexity or a difficult reporting relationship. Outside Top-X an algorithm derived structures and profilers investigated significant monthly changes. The population was reconsidered annually. (F5, pp. 369–371; [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md).)

**Delineate the business in five steps.** (F5, pp. 371–373; [control versus consolidation](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).)

CBS reports a change of direction: previously it grouped legal units to form enterprises; in the method below it identifies enterprises first and then links their legal units. Compare [bottom-up and top-down delineation](../analyses/profiling-approaches.md#bottom-up-from-legal-units-to-enterprises). F5 gives relative timing, not a precise changeover year.

1. Construct the control group. The trade register recorded sole ownership, not every controlling interest; tax units, annual reports, market disclosures and the group itself could fill gaps.
2. Test the consolidation perimeter separately. For instance, part of a joint venture can be consolidated without being controlled; some controlled entities can be excluded from a particular consolidation.
3. Test operating segments top-down. Can the segment make production decisions, sell outside the group, and provide meaningful statistics? Combine an internal service with the activities it serves, or split a segment if its autonomous components can report separately.
4. Link legal units to the resulting enterprises and establish activity and size. Where enterprise accounts are unavailable, legal-unit data are a fallback; simple addition is not the same as consolidation.
5. Write up the reasoning, distribute it to users and implement it in the register. A successor profiler needs to know *why* the delineation was chosen.

**Maintain between reviews.** Daily trade and monthly tax feeds generated signals, with safeguards against overwriting certain profiled data. Profilers checked batch results; approved changes entered monthly frames. The case aimed at active annual profiles subject to resources, in addition to reactive work. For multinationals, staff checked foreign parents and decision centres and repaired discrepancies in the EuroGroups Register. A national collection unit was not automatically the resident slice of a global segment. (F5, pp. 371–374; [European profiling](../concepts/european-profiling.md).)

**Find errors while something can still be done.** ConGO brought profilers, account managers and statistical analysts together after inconsistencies were being discovered only during national accounts compilation, sometimes after publication. A tool compared microdata across programmes, frequencies and periods, including survey and tax data. A discrepancy prompted investigation, not automatic correction: analysts consulted the profile, account managers coordinated with the business, and profilers corrected structural errors. One tension was that the profiler needed today's structure while the analyst needed consistent figures for an earlier period. (F5, pp. 374–377; [change and quality](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md).)

**Do not confuse proposals with practice.** Combining all three roles in one person, separating statistical importance from the need for active profiling in two lists, and broader “ConGO-light” monitoring were proposed or being studied. The case identifies a trade-off in a single contact knowing everything: continuity becomes harder when that person is unavailable. (F5, pp. 377–379.)

## Later evidence: wider checks and respondent-facing reconciliation

[Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§6.4.1–6.4.3, pp. 45–48, reports that CBS copied the tool to a separate environment for annual checks outside the LCU population. This is evidence of broader monitoring, not proof that all earlier ConGO proposals were adopted. Within the LCU, requiring explanations for too many alerts had distracted from important cases. CBS reduced the active rules; for statistical year 2023 it retained 18 at enterprise level and 21 at group level.

Account managers also use three templates to compare profit and loss, reconcile turnover/production across three surveys, and check investments against balance-sheet movements. The templates expose raw, cleaned and prior-period figures so the company can recognise its own returns and explain adjustments. A template edit does **not** alter the source: confirmed adjustments are prepared separately. See [who owns the correction](../concepts/lcu-coordination.md) and [profile quality](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md).

## Profiling organization reported in 2020

The [Eurostat 2020 manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §6.B.1, pp. 96–97 and Table 1, describes CBS's Top-X as about 1,900 groups in prose (the table rounds it to approximately 2,000). The prose identifies 300 ConGO groups, while the table gives 360. ConGO combined profiling with account management and cross-statistic consistency work; remaining Top-X groups were maintained by the business-register department, and groups outside Top-X were largely algorithmically derived with manual review of significant changes. The manual's counts and staffing are a snapshot and are not reconciled across its prose and table.

## Establishment detail in industry statistics

In the [2025 Wiesbaden presentation](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slide 13 uses the Netherlands as an example of establishment-based industry statistics while discussing a proposal to produce statistics on both an enterprise and an establishment basis. It names no series or coverage period. This is evidence about the [choice of unit for an output](../units/activity-and-location.md), not a change to the top-down method CBS describes above for forming enterprises.
