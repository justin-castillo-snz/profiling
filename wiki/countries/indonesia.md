# Indonesia

## From a ranked list to an approved group structure

[UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F4, pp. 367–369, and Annex C6, pp. 325–334. F4 describes the workflow; C6 recounts the first years of development from 2013. Neither establishes today's coverage.

1. **Find a starting population.** BPS used a magazine's revenue-ranked top 100 groups. The ranking selects candidates; it does not prove which legal units belong to them. Investigators preferred the latest company annual report, then financial statements, then company website detail, then other sources such as exchange or ministry material. (F4, p. 368; [case selection](../concepts/case-selection.md).)
2. **Build the network.** Classify units, prioritise subsidiaries, flag duplicates, check directory coverage and repair incomplete or inconsistent fields. Subject-matter profilers could consult directories across sectors. Group supervisors approved additions, edits, duplicate resolutions and creation or removal of relationships; a monitoring supervisor oversaw targets and quality across subject areas. Thus recording a candidate relationship and approving it were different jobs. (F4, pp. 367–369, fig. F4.1.)
3. **Test unit concepts against actual businesses.** During early development, field checks complemented desk research. A bank branch, cash office, hotel and property development do not imply the same production structure. BPS found it needed industry-specific ways to recognise [enterprises, establishments and supporting units](../units/activity-and-location.md). Groups could have up to seven subsidiary levels; rapid ownership changes dated the evidence quickly, and subsidiary addresses were hard to establish. (C6, pp. 326–328.)

**Lesson reported by BPS.** Set size limits so economically important units remain manageable, consider over- and undercoverage, maintain business relationships, and allow exceptions rather than forcing each group into one pattern. In 2014 work concentrated on the largest 100 groups; in 2015 subject-matter teams participated in ongoing maintenance. (C6, activities in 2013–2015.) See the [profiling workflow](../concepts/profiling-workflow.md).
