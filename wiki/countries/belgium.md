# Belgium

## National Bank of Belgium: establish the perimeter before balancing the flows

The National Bank of Belgium (NBB) established its LCU in 2021. Statistics Belgium is responsible for profiling; a cooperation agreement enables the two institutions to share data. The bank's collection department performs first-line validation, while the LCU performs second-line statistical checks. These are **not** three names for the same function. [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §6.5, pp. 48–56; [profiling scope](../concepts/profiling-workflow.md).

1. Identify linked resident and non-resident units with Statistics Belgium. Use the register and Belgian registration numbers to link administrative and survey observations.
2. Convert microdata into comparable macroeconomic concepts and aggregate from legal units to the group. First establish activities, branches, special-purpose entities, and the economic owner of goods and intellectual property. Only then test supply and use for each legal unit and for the group.
3. If the underlying microdata need correction, route the case to the collection department or firm. If a conceptual adjustment is required, send the receiving statistical teams estimates before and after reconciliation. Routine questions can go through the collector; in-depth business questions are coordinated by the account manager. (§§3.1, 6.5, figs 7–9; [unit of analysis](../units/observation-and-reporting.md), [correction mandates](../concepts/lcu-coordination.md).)

**2025 Eurostat overview.** Eurostat summarises the NBB workflow as a loop from micro to macro and back: profiling (jointly with the NSI), compilation from legal unit to group, analysis and validation, then dissemination, with corrections at micro or legal-unit level. The slides give 2021 as the start year, matching the guidelines. ([Paper](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-paper-2025.md), pp. 8–9; [slides](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md), 7, 19.)
