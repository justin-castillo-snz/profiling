# Observation and reporting units

The unit statistics describe need not be the unit that answers the questionnaire. A profiler therefore needs to record **who reports, for which operations, for what period and at what level of detail**. [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §8.5.1 and fig. 8.5; §6.6.4, para. 6.126.

- **Statistical unit:** the entity about which statistics are compiled, for example an enterprise or establishment.
- **Observation unit:** the entity about which values are actually obtained. Its boundaries matter when comparing survey and administrative data.
- **Reporting unit:** the entity supplying those values. One office can report for several workplaces; its address is not evidence that all the jobs are there.

**Unit of analysis in consistency work.** [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§4.6, 4.8, 5.1: link identifiers through the register, then establish what the returns actually cover. A monthly local-unit return, annual consolidated enterprise accounts and tax data for legal units need not have the same perimeter. A common identifier enables linkage; it does not establish that the values can be added. [Denmark](../countries/denmark.md) checks at the reporting legal unit; [Sweden](../countries/sweden.md) uses dated frame links across observation units; [Belgium](../countries/belgium.md) checks legal units and the group.

**Collection and compilation can use different units.** The [2025 Wiesbaden slides](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slides 8–9 and 12–14, contrast enterprise-based surveys, including in the [UK](../countries/united-kingdom.md), with administrative data at legal-unit level. They also report a World Bank example of establishment-based census collection followed by compilation for enterprises. Neither is a rule for how to form an enterprise. Knowing where the books or employment records are held tells a profiler whom to ask; it does not determine the activity or location those figures describe. See [choice of statistical unit](activity-and-location.md).

**Applications.** [Canada](../countries/canada.md) uses business language such as cost centre to find out which figures exist. [Switzerland](../countries/switzerland.md) maps a central personnel delivery to individual local units. [The UK](../countries/united-kingdom.md) checks links between enterprises, reporting units and local units as well as the validity of survey contacts. [South Africa](../countries/south-africa.md) checks which surveys and observation units a revised profile affects. An updated contact list alone would not answer any of these coverage questions. (Annex F2, F6–F8.)

**Collection entities derived from the profile (Canada, 2018).** Statistics Canada's EPMs derive collection entities from the profiled structure, keep a survey inventory per enterprise, and offer combined questionnaires and a spreadsheet covering several entities. Standard central collection treats surveys independently, so a respondent may be called by several surveys. ([Voorburg paper](../sources/voorburg-statcan-epm-2018.md), §§3.2–3.3.)

**Allocating reported data to lower units (Canada, 2015, historical).** When a business reports once for the enterprise or company, Statistics Canada's register stores revenue and employment factors to distribute data to establishments and locations; the allocation matters most for national-level reporting. Customised spreadsheets for the largest businesses were costly and delayed coherence checks, and a metadata-based reporting tool was planned. ([IBSP overview](../sources/statcan-ibsp-overview-2015.md), §§4, 8.)

**European profile versus national observation unit.** The [2020 Eurostat manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§1.C–1.D and 4.A, separates global analytical units (GENs) from automatically projected TENTs and nationally delineated ENTs. A TENT is informative and provisional; it is not automatically a national survey or reporting unit. Partner NSIs retain authority to reshape it. In that manual's data collection, every unit's reference year and the measure's source and type matter: for example, employment may be headcount or full-time equivalent, at a date or averaged over a year; turnover should retain its concept and currency. See [European profiling](../concepts/european-profiling.md).

**One channel for a group's reporting (INE Spain proposal, 2025).** The INE authors report that a single multinational group can receive over 2,000 questionnaires a year and propose MNEData: a secure platform, managed by the LCU, where the group uploads a predefined variable set for all its resident legal units, initially for global production arrangements and trade in services. Claimed benefits are consistency, lower burden and near-real-time data; risks include commitment, security, technical compatibility and agreeing a variable dictionary. It is a proposal with an indicative 2026 pilot, not an implemented method. ([INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §6; [Spain](../countries/spain.md).) *Analyst interpretation:* it addresses who reports and through what channel, which is separate from how the group's units are delineated.
