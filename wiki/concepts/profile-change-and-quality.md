# What changes when a profile changes?

**Separate a real event from a correction to the register.** A change of owner can end a tax registration without ending the producing enterprise. Opening a local unit can change employment distribution without creating a new enterprise. A merger, takeover, break-up or split-off in the legal records is a signal to investigate, not proof of an equivalent statistical birth or death. Record predecessor/successor links and dates to reconstruct the event. [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), paras 7.35–7.43.

**Distinguish when an event happened from when it was learned and applied.** The live register can change after a survey frame is frozen. A new delineation can change the apparent level of an industry, region or time series without a corresponding change in output. Notify affected programmes, preserve the old view and explain the transition; do not silently compare two different scopes. (Paras 7.19–7.25; Box 7.3; Annex F5, pp. 371, 375–377.)

**Check structure as well as totals.** [Canada](../countries/canada.md) checks allocated turnover and employment against legal-level values, then compares them with other statistical sources. [The Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md) uses inconsistencies across programmes as leads: an analyst can trace a discrepancy to the profile and request a correction before the next collection. [Switzerland](../countries/switzerland.md) can have a plausible group total but a missing workplace; it checks coverage and regional distribution. [The UK](../countries/united-kingdom.md) checks for duplicate local units and stale reporting links.

**What does “vertical” mean here?** In the [UN Canadian case](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md) (Annex F2, p. 367), *vertical coherence* tests allocated operating-unit values against tax values higher in the business hierarchy; *horizontal coherence* compares the allocations with other administrative, survey or programme data. In the [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md) (§4.1), *vertical consistency* means checks **within a domain**, such as comparing successive years, and *horizontal consistency* means comparisons across surveys and sources. Name the comparison (up a hierarchy, through time or across sources) rather than relying on the label.

**Coherent need not mean equal.** Eurostat (§§4.1–4.2, 4.6–5.3) treats a rule failure as a question. Check units, consolidation, accounting basis, vintage and period before interpreting it. For example, two enterprises selling to each other can have combined turnover greater than their group's consolidated turnover. Preserve the reported value, transformations and comparison value; annualise shorter periods only if coverage matches. Start with a few informative checks; investigate promptly where subannual data are comparable. [Sweden](../countries/sweden.md) separates conceptual differences from avoidable reporting errors; [Italy](../countries/italy.md) traced one discrepancy to a branch; [Poland](../countries/poland.md) distinguishes missing reports from measured differences.

**Assess whether the result was usable.** [South Africa](../countries/south-africa.md) distinguishes the quality of the investigation from the relevance, accuracy, timeliness, accessibility, interpretability and coherence of its product. A completed interview or a processed change is not by itself evidence that survey staff can use or explain the new profile. (Annex F6, figs F6.2–F6.5.)

**Group continuity is a judgement, not a registration event.** The UN sets out continuity rules for enterprise groups: for large all-resident groups, profiling may inform the decision; for small groups, automated procedures from administrative and register information can be used (§7.86). Only substantial annual changes count, and sudden change in large groups is rare; a change of group head is one example. A key factor is how the group itself sees its continuity, and there must be strong reasons to go against it (§§7.87–7.88). The factors weighed are the global group head, member enterprises and main locations, principal activity (ISIC), employment and intangible assets (§7.89). If all change, discontinuity is obvious; otherwise the changes are weighed. A new group head that changes the whole profile can mean a new group even when the head's identity is unchanged (§§7.90–7.91). A truncated group's continuity follows that of its multinational group (§7.84); multinational continuity is to be coordinated internationally, for example through the EuroGroups Register identity number (§7.85; see [European profiling](european-profiling.md)). Pages 171–172 of the UN text.

**Large-unit errors may need a special procedure.** Errors in large and economically significant units can substantially affect statistics, so the UN says special handling may need to be negotiated with users, notably survey managers and national accounts, and fully documented if implemented. It names two options: a dedicated profiling team, or an authority such as the SBR manager with the final say on corrections (§7.126). It also advises recording each error's source, type and dates so users can access both corrected and uncorrected data (§7.127). The Guidelines do not say which option is preferable; that choice is a design question for a team like Complex Business Support.

**Metadata should describe the profiling process.** Production-process metadata should cover SBR profiling and improvement surveys, and the procedures used to resolve conflicting source information (§§10.47–10.48). The quality of different unit populations matters to different users: accurate activity and size codes for large units are paramount for value-added estimates, whereas business-demography indicators depend more on small units (Chapter 10, quality criteria discussion).

**Compare how the units were formed, not just how they are labelled.** The [2025 Wiesbaden presentation](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slides 10–11, reports an EU comparability issue between enterprises formed by automatic and intensive profiling. It does not show how large or widespread the differences were or establish which method was better. **Analyst implication:** when comparing counts or industry results across periods or populations, record the delineation method as well as the unit, and check whether membership or coverage differs before interpreting the change.

**Profile evidence has its own reference period and accounting meaning.** The [2020 Eurostat manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§4.C–4.E, calls for consistent reference years and explicit metadata for source, currency, employment type and turnover concept. It treats consolidated turnover as desirable for GEG/GEN comparisons, distinguishes financial-activity measures from non-financial turnover, and notes that employment is additive (at least for full-time equivalents) under the stated method whereas turnover may not be. Group-published revenue, sales, turnover and an unknown accounting line are not interchangeable without validation. These are requirements and recommendations in the manual's European profiling exercise, not Stats NZ definitions.
