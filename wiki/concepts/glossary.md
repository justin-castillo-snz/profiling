# Glossary of profiling terms

Technical terms, acronyms and phrases used in the wiki, in alphabetical order. Each entry gives a short definition **as the cited source uses it**, the source, and the wiki page that explains it. This glossary is analyst-compiled from the ingested sources only; it adds no outside definitions and is not NSO policy.

**Reading it.** Many terms come from the 2020 European manual and do not map one-to-one onto other systems. Where sources use a term differently, both usages are shown and the difference is flagged. Terms the wiki uses but no ingested source defines are listed at the end.

## A–C

**Ancillary unit** — A unit (for example a legal employer or service company) working solely for other units of the group. The UN cautions that treating it as a separate producer can misrepresent the group's activity; INE Spain excludes ancillary and integrated units from a group's total output. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§4.3–4.4; [INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §4.3.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).

**Automatic profiling** — Algorithmic derivation of structure for smaller or less complex groups, with analyst review of selected cases. A *method/intensity* label, not a way of delineating units. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §1.B.7; Table 1.) See [Profiling approaches](../analyses/profiling-approaches.md#rule-based-automatic-profiling).

**Consolidation boundary** — The units included in a set of accounts. It answers a different question from control: a partly consolidated joint venture need not belong to the control group. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F5.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).

**Control perimeter** — The set of legal units under the control of a group, established before enterprises are delineated. In the Spanish account it is fixed from consolidated and individual accounts and includes units missing from registers (branches, special purpose entities, subsidiaries); the European manual distinguishes control from accounting consolidation. The sources describe how it is established rather than giving a single formal definition. ([INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §4.3; [Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), Chapter 2.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md#perimeter-evidence-and-structural-edge-cases).

**ConGO** (*Consistentie Grote Ondernemingen*) — Statistics Netherlands' unit and process for cross-statistic consistency checks on about 300 large groups (historical figures). ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F5.) See [Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md).

## D–G

**Delineation** — Deciding what the statistical unit is: which legal units combine into an enterprise, and how its boundary is drawn. Distinct from activity classification, which assigns an industry code to an already-defined unit. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §4.33.) See [Activity and location](../units/activity-and-location.md) and [Profiling approaches](../analyses/profiling-approaches.md).

**ENT** (national enterprise) — In the European manual, the enterprise finally delineated by a partner NSI; it need not match the global enterprise (GEN) or the temporary enterprise (TENT) one-to-one. In the INE paper, "ENT" is the enterprise as "the smallest combination of legal units". ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§1.C–1.D, 4.A; [INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §2.1.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**Enterprise** — The producer with autonomy over its use of resources; one legal unit can suffice and several may need to be combined. Wording varies: the UN stresses autonomy; Eurostat stresses allocation of current resources; Allen's 2025 slides give "the smallest combination of legal units with a degree of decision-making autonomy" and note slight differences among SNA 2008, ISIC Rev. 4 and EU formulations without settling them. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§4.3–4.4; [Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§3.A–3.E; [Wiesbaden 2025](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slide 6.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).

**Enterprise group** (EG, GEG) — Legal units linked under common control, followed to the controlling head rather than the immediate parent. "EG" is Stats SA's label (a group starting from an SA-incorporated holding company); "GEG" is the European/INE label for the whole (global) group. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §4.3; [Stats SA presentation](../sources/wbg-stats-sa-profiling-presentation-2025.md), slide 12; [INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §2.1.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).

**EGR** (EuroGroups Register) — The SBR of multinational groups in Europe, built by Eurostat and national offices by pooling microdata on legal units, relationships, enterprises and groups; also the source of the group identity number used for continuity. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), Box 2.8; [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §7.85.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**EPM** (Enterprise Portfolio Manager / Management Program) — Statistics Canada's programme in which one manager is the single contact for profiling, collection and other help for about 30 large complex enterprises. ([Voorburg paper](../sources/voorburg-statcan-epm-2018.md), §§1–3; earlier, 2015 description: [IBSP overview](../sources/statcan-ibsp-overview-2015.md), §8.) In the 2025 deck, 10 EPMs cover 230 ultimate parent enterprises and the programme also includes analysts and coherence staff ([deck](../sources/statcan-epm-nz-lcu-meeting-2025.md), pp. 3–4). **Enterprise Report** — StatCan document through which profiling findings are shared with other programmes (deck, pp. 12–13). **Prescription order** — Chief Statistician's order authorizing mandatory profiling by EPMs and BR profilers (deck, p. 6). See [LCU coordination](lcu-coordination.md) and [Canada](../countries/canada.md).

**ESBRs** — The European project (2013–2020) continuing earlier EGR and profiling work: an agreed methodology, roles for countries profiling the same multinational, and a secure data-sharing platform. Status after the 2024 text not verified. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md); [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md).) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**Establishment** — An enterprise or part at one location with one non-ancillary productive activity, or a principal activity accounting for most value added (European equivalent: local KAU). Allen's slides use a shorthand, "part of an enterprise defined by activity and location". ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§4.5–4.6; [Wiesbaden 2025](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slide 6.) See [Activity and location](../units/activity-and-location.md).

**EWS** (Early Warning System) — A voluntary European system for early exchange on major events affecting multinational groups, dating from 2017. An alert can trigger investigation; it is not an approved change to a national enterprise. ([Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§1.3, 2.2–2.3; [INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §3.1; [Eurostat 2025 paper](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-paper-2025.md), pp. 3–5, which adds that it is non-legislative, can lead to monitoring of a group for a year or more, and that closed cases are published as methodological summary notes.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**GDC** (global decision centre) — Where strategic group decisions are made, regardless of legal position or location. It determines which NSI coordinates European profiling and can differ from the GGH. The UN says it cannot be set by an operational rule and "can be established during profiling". ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§1.B–1.D; [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex A.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md#european-manual-global-delineation-and-autonomy).

**GEN** (global enterprise) — The European manual's global analytical enterprise, delineated top-down by autonomy within the global group. A technical, global delineation, not a national enterprise boundary. Note: the INE paper counts groups (GEG), not GENs. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§1.C–1.D, 3.A–3.E.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**GGH** (global group head) — The top of the ownership/control tree or legal hierarchy. Not to be assumed identical to the GDC; INE reports GGH and UCI often but not always coincide. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§1.B–1.D; [INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §2.1.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).

## I–L

**Intensive, light and follow-up profiling** — *Method/intensity* labels in the 2020 European manual: intensive = comprehensive dialogue including a meeting with the group; light = desk research with absent or limited contact; follow-up = updating an earlier profile. They do not set a selection threshold. **Source-specific:** Spain uses "intensive profiling" for perimeter plus enterprise delineation; the UN's Swiss case uses "light profiling" differently. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §1.B.7; [Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §6.6.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md) and [Profiling workflow](profiling-workflow.md).

**IPT** (Interactive Profiling Tool) — Eurostat's secure collaborative platform for European profiling, holding linked, structured profile records. Current access and participation are not verified. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §1.C.1.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**ISIC** — International Standard Industrial Classification, used to assign principal activity. ([Wiesbaden 2025](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slides 15–16.) See [Activity and location](../units/activity-and-location.md).

**KAU** (kind-of-activity unit) — An enterprise or part engaged in one productive activity, or one whose principal activity accounts for most value added; it need not occupy one site or equal one legal unit. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§4.5–4.6.) See [Activity and location](../units/activity-and-location.md).

**LCU** (large cases unit; "Large Case Unit" in some sources) — An organizational setting that coordinates the statistical treatment of economically significant businesses so statistics are consistent or explainable. It is not a statistical unit. ([Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§1.2, 2.1–2.3.) See [LCU coordination](lcu-coordination.md).

**Legal unit** (LEU, LeU) — The legally recognised person or entity. Its registration or accounts identify a legal boundary, not necessarily an autonomous producer. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §4.3.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).

**Local unit** — An enterprise or part operating at or from one location; geography is defined for the statistical purpose. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§4.5–4.6.) See [Activity and location](../units/activity-and-location.md).

## M–S

**MNEnet** — A European network of coordinators sharing knowledge and relaying it to statistical domains. ([Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§1.3, 2.2–2.3.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**NSI / NSO** — National statistical institute (the term used in the Eurostat sources) / national statistics office. The wiki uses them for the office doing the work; no ingested source defines the abbreviations.

**Observation unit and reporting unit** — The *observation unit* is the entity about which values are actually obtained; the *reporting unit* is the entity supplying them. Both may differ from the *statistical unit*. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §8.5.1.) See [Observation and reporting](../units/observation-and-reporting.md).

**Principal activity** — The activity used to assign a unit's industry code; the chosen class must be consistent with its parent levels, using value added where available, and need not account for a majority of value added. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), glossary.) See [Activity and location](../units/activity-and-location.md#activity-classification-after-delineation).

**Profile** — A maintained description of a business's structure and reporting arrangements. ([Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§1.2, 2.1.) See [LCU coordination](lcu-coordination.md).

**Profiling** — Depends on the source. Eurostat: a method to analyse and maintain a group's legal, operational and accounting structure, establish statistical units and their links, and support collection. UN: work within the broader SBR, extending to agreeing reporting arrangements. The Belgian bank's workflow uses it for the first phase only. Scopes differ; they are not rival definitions. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§1.B.7–1.B.8; [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§6.120–6.127; [Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §6.5.) See [Profiling workflow](profiling-workflow.md).

**SBR** (statistical business register) — The maintained register of statistical businesses, units and relationships. Profiling is an investigative method used when routine register sources cannot represent a complex business. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Chapters 6, 11.) See [Profiling workflow](profiling-workflow.md).

**SPE** (special purpose entity) — A legal unit the European manual includes in the perimeter while retaining its control links; also a typical unit missing from registers in the Spanish perimeter work. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§2.D–2.E; [INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §4.3.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md#perimeter-evidence-and-structural-edge-cases).

**Statistical unit** — The entity about which statistics are compiled, for example an enterprise or establishment. Enterprise group, enterprise, KAU, local unit and establishment are distinct units; a change in one does not automatically imply a change in another. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §8.5.1.) See [Observation and reporting](../units/observation-and-reporting.md).

## T–Z

**TENT** (temporary enterprise) — A technical country projection of a GEN, informative and provisional; a proposal that a partner NSI may confirm, split, combine or reshape. ([Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§1.C–1.D, 4.A.) See [European profiling](european-profiling.md).

**Top-down and bottom-up** — *Delineation:* top-down forms enterprises from the group by autonomy and then links legal units; bottom-up groups resident legal units into enterprises. *Activity:* in Allen's 2025 slides "top-down" is the ISIC method for finding principal activity. *Consistency work:* the LCU guidelines use top-down for working from the group, later in the process. These are different uses; see [Profiling approaches](../analyses/profiling-approaches.md). ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §4.33; [Wiesbaden 2025](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slides 15–16; [Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §1.2.)

**Top-X** — Statistics Netherlands' historical population of about 1,900 large groups (about 2,000 in the 2020 table) selected for profiling. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F5; [Eurostat 2020](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §6.B.1.) See [Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md).

**Truncated (national) group** — The resident part of a multinational group; it may be as small as one unit, and its continuity follows that of the multinational group. Stats SA's resident-only group view is similar in effect, but its sources do not use the term (analyst interpretation). ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§7.51–7.84.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md#multinational-limits-and-group-types).

**UCI** (ultimate controlling institutional unit) — The unit at the top of control; can be a natural person, public administration, sovereign fund or a resident or non-resident unit. Its relation to the UN's "controlling head" is not worked out in the sources. ([INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §2.1.) See [Group, control and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md#unit-vocabulary-ine-spain-2025).

**Vertical and horizontal coherence / consistency** — The labels differ by source. UN (Canadian case): *vertical coherence* tests allocated operating-unit values against tax values higher in the business hierarchy; *horizontal* compares with other data. Eurostat LCU: *vertical consistency* = checks within a domain such as successive years; *horizontal* = across surveys and sources. Name the comparison rather than relying on the label. ([UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), Annex F2; [Eurostat LCU](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §4.1.) See [Profile change and quality](profile-change-and-quality.md).

## Used in the wiki but not defined in the sources read

FATS, SNA (named as inputs to definitions but not themselves defined), NOGA, SBS, A10 (ten activity categories in INE's selection) and CRA (Canada Revenue Agency, administrative data) appear in source notes without definitions suitable for a glossary entry. Add them when a source defines them.
