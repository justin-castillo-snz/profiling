# Who owns the case, the contact and the correction?

A large cases unit (LCU) coordinates the statistical treatment of economically significant businesses. Its outcome is consistent or explainable statistics. A [profile](profiling-workflow.md) is a maintained description of structure and reporting arrangements; an LCU may produce it, obtain it from another team, or request a revision. [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§1.2, 2.1–2.3, 3.1–3.2.

| Decision | Options documented by Eurostat | Evidence in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Who collects and edits? | A centralised team can collect once and deliver edited records; a decentralised team can investigate across surveys while domains retain their data. Reporting line alone does not determine the mandate. | [Ireland](../countries/ireland.md) supplies a clean unit file; [Denmark](../countries/denmark.md) follows up corrections implemented by domains; [Finland](../countries/finland.md) is classified as decentralised. |
| Who approaches the business? | A named account manager coordinates requests, but is not necessarily the collector, profiler or expert for every question. Review survey contacts, previous explanations, accounts and reporting deadlines before asking again. | [Sweden](../countries/sweden.md) checks with collection first; [Belgium](../countries/belgium.md) routes routine and complex questions differently. |
| Who resolves a discrepancy? | Consult affected domains first. Agree the level of correction (source return, derived data or macro estimate), who acts and when; retain reasons and before/after values. A difference may be legitimate. | [Germany](../countries/germany.md) distinguishes case management and methodology roles; [the Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md) separates respondent-facing reconciliation from edits to source data. |

**Preparing an engagement** (§3.1, pp. 21–25). Identify what the existing information cannot settle. Invite someone who understands the consolidated accounts and bring in operations or tax staff for particular flows. Send an agenda in advance, explain the statistical purpose and confidentiality, and ask questions in business terms. Confirm minutes, actions and outstanding questions with the group. In [Spain](../countries/spain.md), the business is asked to correct a *proposed* delineation rather than build the profile for the office.

**Institutional discovery** (§§2.1–2.3, 3.2). Eurostat suggests mapping existing work first: who already contacts each group, who can see and amend each dataset, which problems reach national accounts too late, and when revisions are possible. Accounting, trade, register and relationship skills need not all be in one team; [Greece](../countries/greece.md) illustrates why headcount need not equal available casework capacity. These are questions for discovery, not prescriptions for Stats NZ.

**Profiling teams are not necessarily LCUs.** The [2020 Eurostat profiling manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§6.B–6.D, says there is no single ideal profiling-team structure: mandates and placement vary with the register, survey and data-collection systems. It describes teams in business-register departments, statistical production areas and combined settings, with different divisions of profiling, data collection and validation. Its LCU discussion says an LCU may sit within or outside a profiling team; the two should coordinate because both monitor large, complex groups, but the activities are not synonymous (§6.C.2). This 2020 guidance complements the later [LCU guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), which focus more directly on consistency work and correction authority.

## A reported portfolio-manager model: Statistics Canada EPM (2018)

One Enterprise Portfolio Manager per enterprise (about 30 each, 300 enterprises) is the single contact for profiling, collection and other help, supported by analysts and separate coherence specialists, about 60% of EPM time going to profiles. The paper claims lower burden and better data, but offers only a 95% response rate as evidence. ([Voorburg paper](../sources/voorburg-statcan-epm-2018.md), §§3–5; [Canada](../countries/canada.md).) Against the Eurostat table above, this is a case where the account manager *is* also profiler and collection coordinator; Eurostat notes that need not be so. *Analyst interpretation:* resourcing at this scale (about 19 staff) and the legal basis for mandatory profiling questions are Canadian conditions.

**2025 update (Statistics Canada deck for NZ).** The programme is described as 10 EPMs, 6–7 analysts and 7 coherence staff covering 230 enterprises, with four areas: relationships, profiling and BR maintenance, response management, and coherence. A prescription order signed by the Chief Statistician makes profiling activities mandatory; EPMs may restrict other agency contact with a respondent at its request; hiring favours accounting, negotiation and organisation skills (coherence staff: accounting plus programming); and lessons stress management support up to the DG, a cross-survey data warehouse and clear guidelines. The deck is self-reported and gives no cost or baseline. ([Deck](../sources/statcan-epm-nz-lcu-meeting-2025.md), pp. 3–6, 17–18, 22–24.) NZ's own questions (reaching deeper detail with businesses, sourcing structure changes, recruitment) are listed there as unanswered.

## Eurostat's 2025 overview of European LCUs

Eurostat's Wiesbaden paper lists six LCU purposes (group relationships, EWS, central coordination of collection, consistency checks and validation, profiling, knowledge exchange) and draws on the draft *guidelines on data consistency checks for MNE groups* (consortium of DK, IE, NL, SE). Its five principles are prioritisation, communication at the right level of detail, coherence versus consistency, early timing and automated tools. **Coherence versus consistency:** not every inconsistency between sources needs correcting, because concepts and reference periods differ; this is consistent with the Eurostat guidance above that a difference may be legitimate. The slides add that LCUs are placed variously (national accounts, or business and trade statistics) and need organisational change away from stovepipes. ([Paper](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-paper-2025.md), pp. 3–8; [presentation](../sources/wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md), slides 3, 11–16, 20.) The draft is unpublished in this wiki, so the [published edition](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md) remains the authority.

## A reported LCU design: INE Spain (2025)

INE's LCU is a division reporting directly to the presidency, created by ministerial order in 2022, with functions that mix profiling (multiannual intensive plan, monitoring reorganisations), engagement, microdata validation, globalisation analysis and representation in the European LCU network and Early Warning System. It coordinates production units, not replaces them. The authors list integration with traditional production lines ("stove pipes") and balancing follow-up against onboarding new groups as continuing operational challenges. ([INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §§3.2, 5; [Spain](../countries/spain.md).) The presentation's timeline gives LCU start years for other European offices as the authors state them; treat these as secondary reporting, not verified by those offices ([presentation](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-presentation-2025.md)).
