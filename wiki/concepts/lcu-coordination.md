# Who owns the case, the contact and the correction?

A large cases unit (LCU) coordinates the statistical treatment of economically significant businesses. Its outcome is consistent or explainable statistics. A [profile](profiling-workflow.md) is a maintained description of structure and reporting arrangements; an LCU may produce it, obtain it from another team, or request a revision. [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§1.2, 2.1–2.3, 3.1–3.2.

| Decision | Options documented by Eurostat | Evidence in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Who collects and edits? | A centralised team can collect once and deliver edited records; a decentralised team can investigate across surveys while domains retain their data. Reporting line alone does not determine the mandate. | [Ireland](../countries/ireland.md) supplies a clean unit file; [Denmark](../countries/denmark.md) follows up corrections implemented by domains; [Finland](../countries/finland.md) is classified as decentralised. |
| Who approaches the business? | A named account manager coordinates requests, but is not necessarily the collector, profiler or expert for every question. Review survey contacts, previous explanations, accounts and reporting deadlines before asking again. | [Sweden](../countries/sweden.md) checks with collection first; [Belgium](../countries/belgium.md) routes routine and complex questions differently. |
| Who resolves a discrepancy? | Consult affected domains first. Agree the level of correction (source return, derived data or macro estimate), who acts and when; retain reasons and before/after values. A difference may be legitimate. | [Germany](../countries/germany.md) distinguishes case management and methodology roles; [the Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md) separates respondent-facing reconciliation from edits to source data. |

**Preparing an engagement** (§3.1, pp. 21–25). Identify what the existing information cannot settle. Invite someone who understands the consolidated accounts and bring in operations or tax staff for particular flows. Send an agenda in advance, explain the statistical purpose and confidentiality, and ask questions in business terms. Confirm minutes, actions and outstanding questions with the group. In [Spain](../countries/spain.md), the business is asked to correct a *proposed* delineation rather than build the profile for the office.

**Institutional discovery** (§§2.1–2.3, 3.2). Eurostat suggests mapping existing work first: who already contacts each group, who can see and amend each dataset, which problems reach national accounts too late, and when revisions are possible. Accounting, trade, register and relationship skills need not all be in one team; [Greece](../countries/greece.md) illustrates why headcount need not equal available casework capacity. These are questions for discovery, not prescriptions for Stats NZ.

**Profiling teams are not necessarily LCUs.** The [2020 Eurostat profiling manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §§6.B–6.D, says there is no single ideal profiling-team structure: mandates and placement vary with the register, survey and data-collection systems. It describes teams in business-register departments, statistical production areas and combined settings, with different divisions of profiling, data collection and validation. Its LCU discussion says an LCU may sit within or outside a profiling team; the two should coordinate because both monitor large, complex groups, but the activities are not synonymous (§6.C.2). This 2020 guidance complements the later [LCU guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), which focus more directly on consistency work and correction authority.
