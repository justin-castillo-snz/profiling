# Selecting cases and deciding when to reprofile

**Economic importance is not the same as need for a profile.** A stable, high-turnover enterprise can matter greatly to estimates but be easy to represent. Conversely, an acquisition can make the old reporting structure unreliable overnight. The UN suggests choosing the population and a feasible review cycle while reserving capacity for major events. Its three-to-four-year programme is an example, not a standard. [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), paras 6.122–6.125; §7.2.5 and Box 7.2.

- **Proactive** review revisits a portfolio or tests the structure periodically. **Reactive** review investigates signals such as a merger, conflicting survey coverage or an abrupt change in employment. Either can result in a small correction or a full new profile.
- **Intensity** is a different decision from frequency. [European profiling](european-profiling.md) distinguishes intensive manual, light manual and automatic delineation; a UK desk case or the Swiss annual online profiling survey for its intermediate group should not be assumed equivalent to the European light method.
- **Different selection units lead to different lists.** [Canada](../countries/canada.md) starts with establishments important to a particular industry and geography and then selects their enterprises. [The Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md) scores groups and allows nominations. [Indonesia](../countries/indonesia.md) used a published revenue ranking as a practical starting list; [the UK](../countries/united-kingdom.md) combines size, referrals and activity complexity.
- **Design around the statistical output.** [Switzerland](../countries/switzerland.md) needs regular workplace employment; [Australia](../countries/australia.md) uses triggers for its maintained population; [South Africa](../countries/south-africa.md) uses survey and administrative changes as signals for its profiling population. Frequency, contact mode and inclusion thresholds should be recorded separately.
- **A threshold plus complexity rule (South Africa, 2025).** Stats SA's Large Business Unit selects groups with a complex structure and annual group turnover above R100 million, taking candidates from survey-area requests and ownership directories, and re-profiles each after a year or sooner after major change. The threshold's reference year is not stated. ([Presentation](../sources/wbg-stats-sa-profiling-presentation-2025.md), slides 8–9, 12.)
## Why large and complex units get different treatment

**LCU selection is not the same as selection for reprofiling.** [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), §§2.2, 4.7: a “large case” is significant for national statistics, not necessarily above a universal employment threshold or even foreign-owned. An office can maintain a portfolio, accept issue-based referrals, or do both. Rank size, structural complexity, industry significance and foreign flows; invite nominations from affected domains; use expert review and available capacity for the cut-off. The European EGR top-tier Complexity and Statistical Impact index uses quantile ranks across countries. It is an input to, not a substitute for, national judgement or the Dutch national group score. [Spain](../countries/spain.md) supplements ranking with evidence of reporting difficulties; [Italy](../countries/italy.md) asks domains which groups actually need profiling; [Poland](../countries/poland.md) retains the relevant year's group membership.

The UN text repeatedly differentiates maintenance by size and complexity. These are documented statements, not a prescribed threshold. [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), as cited below.

- **Intensity follows impact.** Intensive profiling with comprehensive contact is *recommended* for large and complex groups, because their data have great impact on aggregates. Smaller and less complex groups may be delineated from published accounts and similar information (§4.51).
- **Cost-efficiency drives the update policy.** Small groups may be updated from administrative sources; for large groups "profiling is usually necessary" (§7.78). Updates to legal and operational units linked to large enterprises come from profiling (§7.113).
- **Changes to large units matter more.** They have more impact on statistics, so potential updates should be checked across sources or by contacting the units. Large units are often surveyed with certainty, so survey feedback is the most common source of updates. Managers of economic surveys and national accounts should be consulted and informed (§7.107). For large units, the timing of an update relative to the survey cycle should be decided case by case with profilers and users; rules for small units may be automated (§7.119).
- **Limited staff time is focused by significance.** Warnings for clerical follow-up are limited so staff concentrate on economically significant units, sometimes called "significance editing" (§10.100). In building a register, the Guidelines also say a country can first maintain the units most relevant to GDP, then automate smaller ones (§11.13).
- **Administrative data fit large units poorly.** Information on large and complex units typically comes from surveys or profiling, small units largely from administrative data (§10.18). VAT-derived turnover can be inconsistent for very large enterprises because of legal organisation, consolidation and global effects; the translation may need adjusting or may not be feasible (§§8.40–8.41). Administrative-to-statistical unit links for complex enterprises rest on profiling results (§6.104).

*Analyst interpretation:* these points support treating "large and complex" as a combination of statistical impact and difficulty of obtaining data from administrative sources, rather than a single size cut-off. See also [change and quality](profile-change-and-quality.md).

## European manual, light and automatic treatment

The [2020 Eurostat manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), §1.B.7, distinguishes intensive manual profiling (comprehensive dialogue and a meeting), light manual profiling (desk research with absent or limited contact), follow-up profiling (updating a previous profile) and automatic profiling (for many smaller groups). These labels describe **method/intensity**, not a uniform selection threshold or a review frequency. Its A/B/C quality matrix relates to group size and complexity and should not be reused as a universal classification. National programmes use their own populations and cycles; the historical examples in [France](../countries/france.md), [Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md) and [UK](../countries/united-kingdom.md) demonstrate different models (§6.B).

## A complexity ranking by industries, regions and size (Canada, 2018)

Statistics Canada ranks BR entities by number of industries, number of regions and relative importance (revenue and employment) in them, updates the ranking every two years, and adjusts the final 300-enterprise portfolio using feedback from subject-matter divisions and national accounts. ([Voorburg paper](../sources/voorburg-statcan-epm-2018.md), §3; [Canada](../countries/canada.md).) Complexity here is domestic (industries and regions), unlike Spain's global index below. The formula is in a cited paper not ingested.

**2025 usage.** A 2025 Statistics Canada deck lists the same three factors (industries, regions, relative size from revenue and employment) and adds program-staff judgement about enterprises that cause measurement issues; the portfolio is 230 ultimate parent enterprises, about 42% multinational. It does not state an update cycle, and says EPMs choose which companies to profile "based on several metrics" without naming them. ([Deck](../sources/statcan-epm-nz-lcu-meeting-2025.md), pp. 4–5, 24.)

## A two-stage complexity index (Spain, 2025)

INE Spain ranks groups twice. Stage 1 uses a composite of employees, turnover, number of countries and number of subsidiaries, measured globally from the EuroGroups Register for Spanish-controlled groups. Stage 2 repeats the ranking on Spain-only activity from the national register, whatever the parent's nationality. The top groups are then taken within each of ten broad activity categories (A10), financial groups are excluded as outside INE business statistics, and the final portfolio size is limited by resources. The portfolio grew from 5 groups (2022) to 20 and then 41; the authors observe that each added group contributes less coverage. ([INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §§4.1–4.2, 4.4.1.)

- **Difference from the Eurostat LCU text above:** that text describes the European ranking and nominations as general options and gives Spain's ranking as including cross-source inconsistencies ([Spain](../countries/spain.md)). The INE paper describes only the index and category balance; it does not mention nominations or inconsistency flags. The two are not contradictory, but they are not the same description.
- **Analyst interpretation:** the A10 balance guards against a portfolio dominated by one industry, whereas the South African rule above uses a turnover threshold plus structural complexity. Both are selection rules for intensive work; neither source shows how well it performs.
