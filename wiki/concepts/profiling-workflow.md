# Profiling a complex business

The practical deliverable is a maintained account of how a business is organised **and how statistics can be collected about it**. A legal chart is a starting point. It does not show by itself who controls production, which sites operate, or where the accounts are held. [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§4.3–4.6, 6.6.4.

## Profiling and the SBR

The [Eurostat 2020 manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md), Foreword and §§1.B.7–1.B.8, defines profiling as a method to analyse and maintain a group's legal, operational and accounting structure, establish statistical units and their links, and support statistical data collection. The [UN Guidelines](../sources/un-guidelines-on-sbr.md), §§6.120–6.127 and 11.34–11.40, describe profiling within the broader work of building and maintaining the statistical business register (SBR).

In short, the **SBR is the maintained register** of statistical businesses, units and relationships; **profiling is an investigative method** used especially when complex businesses cannot be represented adequately by routine register updates. Profiling findings may update the SBR, but profiling can also include evidence gathering, discussion with the business and statistical users, and decisions about data collection. Not every SBR update is profiling, and profiling is not necessarily carried out by a separate team.

**Scope depends on the source.** The UN description above extends to agreeing reporting arrangements. In the [Eurostat LCU Guidelines](../sources/eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md), the [Belgian bank's workflow](../countries/belgium.md) calls the first phase *profiling*: identifying linked resident and non-resident units with Statistics Belgium; investigating economic ownership and reconciling figures follow separately (§6.5). [Spain](../countries/spain.md) uses *intensive profiling* for determining the control perimeter **and** delineating enterprises by operational autonomy (§6.6). These are different scopes for a task, not rival definitions of an enterprise. See [who owns the case](lcu-coordination.md).

| Stage | Decision and evidence | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Scope the case | Identify the change or statistical risk. Decide whether one enterprise needs correction or whether the whole group must be re-examined. | [Canada](../countries/canada.md) considers elapsed time, coherence and how many enterprises a signal affects. |
| Reconstruct the structure | Compare register records and survey history with tax links, public filings, annual accounts and company material. Check their dates and boundaries before treating a mismatch as an error. | [Indonesia](../countries/indonesia.md) has a source hierarchy; [the Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md) checks both control and consolidation. |
| Delineate and classify | Identify autonomous enterprises, their constituent legal units and, where necessary, production units and sites. [Assign activity](../units/activity-and-location.md#activity-classification-after-delineation) to the defined unit; verify geography and size for that same perimeter. | [France](../countries/france.md) splits on operational autonomy; [Canada](../countries/canada.md) tests accounting capability. |
| Resolve reporting gaps | Take a provisional structure and specific unanswered questions to someone who knows the accounts or operations. Agree who can report for what; avoid a visit that merely repeats desk research. | [UK](../countries/united-kingdom.md) desk cases include a call; [Switzerland](../countries/switzerland.md) establishes recurring deliveries. |
| Validate and hand over | Check the proposed structure against totals and survey returns. Discuss likely series or frame effects with users. Record the evidence, decision, dates and remaining limitations; verify the live result. | [South Africa](../countries/south-africa.md) assesses the process and its product; [Canada](../countries/canada.md) checks coherence before and after updates. |

This sequence is a **synthesis**, not a mandated interview script. The UN describes mainly large complex groups, but notes that smaller profiles may be completed from published accounts. Profiler skills include understanding business processes, accounts and survey collection, and negotiating enough detail without excessive burden (paras 6.120–6.127). An LCU is one way of organising the work, not a statistical unit or a prerequisite; [Malaysia](../countries/malaysia.md) illustrates the narrower claim that a system can support profiling.

**Where the work sits.** Chapter 11 presents an example register organisation, explicitly not an international standard. It places profiling specialists alongside staff handling administrative sources, survey feedback and industry classification. It also suggests revisiting the triggers over time: an office might start by scheduling its largest groups, then target those that cause collection difficulties as evidence improves (paras 11.34–11.40). Compare the specific division of responsibilities in [the Dutch ConGO unit](../countries/netherlands.md).

**Skills and training.** The UN recommends that the national statistical office have business-accounting expertise within the SBR or profiling units, to use annual accounts to learn about activity, size and structural changes such as takeovers, mergers and split-offs. It also asks that the office stay involved as accounts become digital (§6.36, Box 6.4, p. 119). Profilers and staff doing frame updates should receive a more intensive training programme than ordinary SBR users, so that profiles of complex structures are completed consistently enough to draw statistical elements from (§11.52). Staff who contact businesses in survey collection may also need training on the SBR interface (§11.53).

**Administrative units and the signal queue.** For complex enterprises, the link between administrative units and statistical units depends on profiling results that identify the related legal, administrative and operational units (§6.104). Annex C3.6 describes a workload module that routes update signals needing manual investigation to analysts or profilers, who implement or cancel the change request. See also [case selection](case-selection.md) for how size and complexity change the approach.

**Open research agenda.** Chapter 12 names analysis of country experiences in profiling large and complex enterprises and enterprise groups, and delineation of multinational groups, as topics needing further work (§12.5). It also links global-value-chain studies to profiling the statistical units of global enterprise groups (§12.6). This indicates that the evidence base is still developing, and that the UN does not claim the existing country accounts are comprehensive.

The [2025 Wiesbaden presentation](../sources/wbg-statistical-units-2025.md), slides 4–5 and 15–19, adds two questions: when do modern production arrangements make establishments less suitable for measuring production and compiling supply and use tables, and what practical guidance is needed to classify multinational enterprises? These are research questions, not an instruction to replace establishments with enterprises.

## European manual profiling in practice

The [2020 Eurostat recommendations manual](../sources/european-business-profiling-2020.md) gives a more operational, dated example of a profile lifecycle. Desk research comes first in **both** intensive and light manual profiling: reconcile register perimeters with group disclosures, annual accounts and other statistical sources; identify the GDC; examine operational segments and form a preliminary delineation (§§2.A–2.D, 4.A).

The manual uses **intensive** for comprehensive dialogue including a meeting with the group. In that case, desk research prepares the discussion and the proposed delineation is examined with the group's representatives. **Light** usually means the desk-based process is carried out without a meeting; limited email or conference-call contact may still validate specific points. In light profiling, the desk analysis nearly determines the delineation. The manual also describes light profiling as a possible fallback if a group will not take part in an intensive process (§§1.B.7, 3.C, 5.A.3). Thus, desk research is not itself a completed “light profile” that must precede intensive profiling; it is the shared starting work, while light and intensive describe different levels of engagement.

**Illustrative working-paper outline—not an official Eurostat template.** A useful desk-profile file could record:

| Part | Information to capture |
|---|---|
| Case and evidence | Group, reference year, source documents and their dates. |
| Group perimeter | Legal units believed to be controlled by the group; uncertain or disputed links. |
| Proposed structure | Candidate enterprises and the legal units allocated to each. |
| Delineation reasoning | Evidence about management, staff, reporting systems, capital, production decisions and transactions relevant to autonomy. |
| Measures | Activity classification, employment and turnover, with reference period, source and measure definition. |
| Questions and status | What desk sources did not resolve, who could confirm it, and whether the case is desk-based, awaiting contact or validated. |

This outline is an analyst proposal for organizing the work. The manual describes the analysis and the data entered into IPT, but does not prescribe a standalone “light profile” document in this format.

For intensive cases, discuss the proposed structure, autonomy, legal-unit allocation and available data with the group. The proposal is then reviewed with statistical users and, for cross-border cases, partner NSIs (§§4.A, 5, 6).

The manual's **autonomy test** is broader than whether a segment has its own activity code. Evidence includes identifiable managers and employees; a reporting system and accounts; responsibility for capital and production resources; and the ability to make operational decisions and transact externally or at arm's length within the group. Segment reports, a separate headquarters or a dedicated reporting team are evidence to assess, not automatic proof of enterprise status (§3.B–3.E). See [group and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md).

### Engaging groups and internal users

The manual recommends early, prepared, coordinated contact: understand the group's reporting cycle and previous NSI contacts, explain the statistical purpose and confidentiality, use business language, and send meeting minutes and actions back for confirmation (§5). It describes direct dialogue as a way to resolve unclear autonomy, perimeter and accounting questions, while warning that repeated or uncoordinated requests can damage credibility. Profilers should work continuously with business-register, EGR, survey, national-accounts and other statistical users (§6.C). These are recommendations from a 2020 European context, not a universal contact protocol.

It identifies a **collective team skillset**, not a requirement that every profiler be expert in everything: communication and negotiation, knowledge of business sectors, accounting and reporting, business statistics, register systems and, for European work, effective communication across countries (§6.A). Organizational forms differ by NSI and should be considered in their national context (§6.B); see [LCU and profiling responsibilities](lcu-coordination.md).

## Interview-based profile cycle with training (Canada, 2018)

Statistics Canada's EPMs interview senior company staff by phone or on site, starting from the CRA legal-entity list, link legal and operating entities, record location address, industry and employees, and confirm the accounting type. Full reviews occur at least every 3 years or on signals. Staff take a two-week course and use a task list, handbook and industry guides. ([Voorburg paper](../sources/voorburg-statcan-epm-2018.md), §§3.2, 4; [Canada](../countries/canada.md).) In 2025 the same programme describes research from earlier Enterprise Reports, administrative data, annual reports and the internet, then mostly virtual validation visits with company accountants, comptrollers and financial officers, BR updates via messages, and Enterprise Reports shared with other programmes and national accounts. ([Deck](../sources/statcan-epm-nz-lcu-meeting-2025.md), pp. 12–13, 24.)

## Intensive profiling and annual follow-up (Spain, 2025)

INE Spain describes a two-phase intensive procedure: (1) establish the control perimeter, including units missing from registers such as branches, special purpose entities and subsidiaries; (2) delineate statistical enterprises and consolidate them. Ancillary and integrated units are excluded from the group's total output, and production for non-resident units gets separate treatment. Contact starts with a formal letter from the INE Presidency to the group's chair or CEO, then a meeting at headquarters; the consolidation team is the preferred contact. ([INE paper](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-paper-2025.md), §4.3; [presentation](../sources/wbg-ine-spain-lcu-presentation-2025.md).)

- **Annual handover calendar (paper §4.3):** group list and global group heads by end March t+1; units not in the register by end April; legal-unit list mid-September; enterprise units to the register mid-October; EuroGroups Register update April t+2. Structural business statistics use the previous year's profiling, so year-t results first appear in statistics published in t+2.
- **Follow-up:** a yearly email asks about perimeter changes, which is cheaper once the structure is stable. This is an instance of the European follow-up profiling above, not a different method.
- **Why intensive rather than automatic (presentation):** the authors say it finds hidden units, delineates by the group's own view of decision autonomy, shows whether output goes within the group or to the market, and separates productive from non-productive units. This is the authors' claim, not a tested comparison.
- **Burden:** the authors report that a single group can receive over 2,000 questionnaires a year and propose a single digital channel (MNEData, not implemented). See [Spain](../countries/spain.md) and [observation and reporting](../units/observation-and-reporting.md).
