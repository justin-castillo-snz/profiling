# The Enterprise Portfolio Management and Coherence Programs; Statistics Canada's LCU (26 Nov 2025)

Slide deck by Statistics Canada for a meeting with a New Zealand LCU team about the Enterprise Portfolio Management (EPM) programme. 24 PDF pages.

- **Original:** `SP - Statcan EPM program overview for NZ LCU Nov 26.pdf`. It is NZ–Canada correspondence, found in the Stats NZ SharePoint folder "Complex Business Support / Methodology" (internal, not publicly accessible).
- **Reading copy:** `raw/md/SP - Statcan EPM program overview for NZ LCU Nov 26/`, not published with this wiki.
- **Issuer and authority:** the national statistical office describing its own programme. It is a presentation aid, not a manual or policy.
- **Reference period:** presented 26 November 2025. The only dated statistics are for 2020 (p4).
- **Units:** ultimate parent enterprise (enterprise group), company (legal and operational structures) and establishment. "Enterprise" here means an ultimate parent group, a Canadian usage kept distinct from other offices' enterprise units (see [group and enterprise](../units/group-and-enterprise.md)).
- **Confidentiality:** aggregates only; no unit-level data. Contact details omitted.

## Evidence guide

Page numbers are PDF pages; printed slide numbers differ (they run 1–17, then jump to 23–30).

| PDF page | What the source says |
|---|---|
| p3 | Programme has 10 Enterprise Portfolio Managers (EPMs), supported by 6–7 analysts and 7 coherence staff. Four areas: manage business relationships; profile structures and maintain the Business Register (BR); response (collection) management; data coherence across enterprises and surveys. |
| p4 | Covers 230 of the largest, most complex enterprises: ultimate parents with about 64,000 establishments across multiple companies; about 42% are multinationals. Under 0.1% of businesses, but in 2020 they accounted for 46% of goods exports, 42% of employment, 28% of corporate value added and 27% of corporate revenues (roughly half of each for MNE activity). |
| p5 | Selection: industrial complexity (number of industries), geographic complexity (number of regions), relative size (revenue and employment) and judgement (staff feedback on enterprises causing measurement issues). A chart compares complexity and size for Top 350, Top 5000, "BR Profiling Critical" and EPM groups; axis definitions are not given in the text. |
| p6 | Legal basis: the Statistics Act mandates collection; a prescription order signed by the Chief Statistician authorizes mandatory profiling activities by EPMs and BR profilers. |
| p7–p11 | Relationship management: one EPM is the single focal point per company; EPMs can restrict agency contact with a respondent at the respondent's request. "Relationship capital" is described as a valued resource. Claimed benefits: 95%+ response, more coherent data, willingness to share structure information (called a "must-have" for profiling), confidentiality waivers, questionnaire testing (e.g. oil and gas, factoryless goods production) and help negotiating scanner data from large retailers. |
| p12–p13 | Profiling: purpose is to learn how complex businesses operate and update the BR and survey frame. Sources: earlier Enterprise Reports, administrative data, annual reports, internet research, and contact with respondents and subject-matter programmes. Structures are validated with company accounting managers, comptrollers and financial officers, mostly in virtual visits. Frame updates go through BR messages and notifications; findings are shared in Enterprise Reports, which also inform national accounts. Many data errors and non-response issues can be resolved in a profile meeting. |
| p14–p15 | Collection management: customising survey "collection entities" (review coverage, verify contacts, merge or split questionnaires); methods EQ, CRT and special cases; non-response and failed-edit follow-up. 95%+ response claimed. |
| p16 | Coherence analysis: checks that survey data agree with auxiliary sources at enterprise, company and establishment levels. Named sources of incoherence: duplication; incorrect coverage or profiling errors; over- or under-reporting (e.g. intercompany transactions); over- or under-imputation; collection issues (units in thousands, entry errors). |
| p17 (slide 23) | Lessons: management support at all levels up to the Director General; a data warehouse for cross-survey, cross-enterprise access linked through the BR taxonomy; clear guidelines because the learning curve is long. |
| p18 | Hiring: EPMs need accounting, negotiation and organisational skills and a friendly, assertive, persistent personality; coherence staff need accounting plus analytical programming (SAS; Python "the next frontier"); succession planning matters. |
| p19 | New Zealand's questions to Statistics Canada: unit structure, roles and internal relationships; recruitment skills; lessons for working with businesses (NZ gets initial interest but struggles to reach more detail); sourcing structural information and changes; whether business process diagrams can be shared; helping a business map its data to survey codes and variables. These are NZ's questions, not answered in the deck. |
| p22–p24 | Roles. EPMs are the primary company contact (autonomous, most with accounting background) and customise collection entities and maintain electronic file transfer vaults. Analysts build survey inventories and synthesise subject-matter responses. In profiling, EPMs decide which companies need profiling "based on several metrics" (unspecified), review inventories and findings before profile meetings, plan virtual visits and update operational and legal structures on the BR according to BR concepts and rules. Analysts research structures and help draft Enterprise Reports. |

## Limitations

- **Slide deck.** No methods, thresholds or decision rules; it supports a spoken presentation. Printed slide numbers skip 18–22, so some slides are absent from the file.
- **Undefined terms:** IBSP, BCP, SMA, SNA, EQ, CRT, "BR taxonomy" and the "several metrics" used to pick profiling cases.
- **Self-reported benefits** (95%+ response, better coherence); no baseline or cost.
- **2020 statistics only;** the 2025 portfolio (230) is not tied to a year-specific count of the 2020 shares.
- **Canadian legal basis.** Applicability to NZ is not verified.
