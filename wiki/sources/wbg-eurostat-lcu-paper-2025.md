# Wiesbaden Group: European experience with Large Cases Units (paper)

Tatiana Mrlianova and Biliana Branska-Lateva (Eurostat), abstract and paper for the 29th Wiesbaden Group meeting, Johannesburg (hosted by Stats SA), 27–31 October 2025. It summarises the European LCU landscape and the in-progress Eurostat guidelines on data consistency checks.

- **Original:** `WBG - Abstract Session 3 S3.1_Eurostat-Abstract-Paper.pdf` (11 pages: abstract p. 1, repeated abstract p. 2, paper pp. 3–11). Obtained from the [Wiesbaden Group CIRCABC library](https://circabc.europa.eu/ui/group/ea13cb47-2205-497e-94db-e812a440b486/library/4ac5d0df-0e83-414f-bfb2-35bb7ac5a0ec).
- **Reading copy:** none made; read directly from the attached PDF.
- **Authority:** Eurostat conference paper. Comparative European guidance, not binding on NSOs and not NZ policy. Unit in focus: the MNE group, with analysis aggregated from legal unit to group.
- **Companion:** [presentation](wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md).

## Evidence guide

| Pages | Content |
|---|---|
| 3 | LCUs, the EuroGroups Register (EGR), Early Warning System (EWS) and Interactive Profiling Tool (IPT) are named as ESS activities on MNE groups. |
| 3–5 | Six LCU purposes: relationships with MNE groups (staff who translate accounting documents into statistical figures; lower respondent burden); EWS activities; central coordination of data collection; consistency checks and validation across business, tax and administrative data; profiling (delineating operational, legal and accounting structure into statistical units); exchange of knowledge. The EWS is described as a structured, voluntary, non-legislative process between national compilers and Eurostat for restructuring events such as mergers and acquisitions. A group can be proposed for European profiling and monitored for a year or more, with the EGR updated. Getting information from the group is the main difficulty. Closed cases are published as methodological summary notes. |
| 5 | Implementation: Sweden 2004, then Ireland; France, Finland, Netherlands 2010; Italy, Denmark, Luxembourg, Norway 2017–18. 18 countries with an operational LCU "as of April 2024"; 13 use IT tools for consistency checks. Legal basis for sharing confidential data: Regulation (EC) 223/2009 and EBS Regulation 2019/2152. Benefits: data quality, national accounts and GNI, policy support. |
| 6 | Challenges: national specificities and resources, MNE complexity, capacity and knowledge gaps. |
| 6–8 | The "upcoming" guidelines on data consistency checks: five aims; drafted by a four-NSI consortium (DK, IE, NL, SE) with contributions from nine NSIs and Eurostat's knowledge collection; six chapters (role of LCUs, institutional arrangements, cooperation and communication, consistency analysis, consistency checks, country experiences). Analysis themes: employment, turnover, trade, production abroad, merchanting, factory-less production, IP transactions; group-selection criteria: total turnover, turnover of the largest enterprise, employment, structural complexity, NACE diversity; GSBPM integration. Five principles: prioritisation; communication at the right level of detail; coherence versus consistency (not every inconsistency needs correcting because concepts and reference periods differ); early timing; automated tools. |
| 8–10 | Country examples: Belgium (supply-and-use approach, micro to macro and back, with profiling jointly with the NSI); Denmark (cross-source turnover and trade checks); Ireland (interactive monthly, quarterly and annual forms); Spain (relationship-based engagement, future centralised automated linking); Italy (web monitoring of M&A for the EWS, single submission point, extending indicators to short-term statistics); Poland (dedicated IT tool). |
| 10–11 | Conclusion: European LCU experience may inform other systems, including in Africa. |

## Limitations and discrepancies

- **Session number:** the paper header says Session 2; the slides say Session 3, as do the filenames. The two are treated here as the same session; the [presentation](wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md) uses "Session 3".
- **Count date:** "18 countries as of April 2024" (p. 5) versus "March 2025" in the [slides](wbg-eurostat-lcu-presentation-2025.md). Unresolved whether the figure was updated or the dating differs.
- **Guideline title:** two titles are used (data consistency checks for MNE groups; implementing data consistency within LCUs). The guidelines are unpublished here and the paper's description is a draft-stage summary; compare the [published-edition note](eurostat-lcu-guidelines.md).
- MNEnet is mentioned in the abstract but not developed in the paper body. The contributing-country list contains typographical errors.
- Country examples are short and self-reported; they are not evidence of how each office forms statistical units.
