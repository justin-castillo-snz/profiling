# Wiesbaden Group: Profiling Processes in Statistics South Africa (presentation)

Howard Shozi (Large Business Unit, Business Register, Statistics South Africa), *Profiling processes in Statistics South Africa*, 14-slide presentation for Session 3 (S3.3), "Globalisation and Profiling of Multinational Enterprises", 29th Wiesbaden Group meeting, Johannesburg, 27–31 October 2025.

- **Original:** `WBG - Presentation Session 3 S3.3_StatsSA-Presentation-1.pdf`. Obtained from the [Wiesbaden Group CIRCABC library](https://circabc.europa.eu/ui/group/ea13cb47-2205-497e-94db-e812a440b486/library/4ac5d0df-0e83-414f-bfb2-35bb7ac5a0ec).
- **Reading copy:** Markdown conversion, not published with this wiki.
- **Companion:** [the abstract](wbg-stats-sa-profiling-abstract-2025.md).
- **Unit:** enterprise group (EG) and its legal units in the South African register. Reference period: not stated; the slides describe current practice at the 2025 meeting.

## Evidence guide

| Slides | What the presentation says |
|---|---|
| 3 | Profiling is a continuous process of analysing the structural characteristics of complex groups of companies (EGs): their legal and operational organisation structure, the economic activities within the group, and data collection and updating of the SBR. |
| 4 | An illustrative group chart (ultimate holding company, direct and indirect subsidiaries, ownership percentages, some foreign subsidiaries). The real entities are deliberately not reproduced in this wiki. |
| 5 | **Profiled:** listed EGs (Johannesburg Stock Exchange), state-owned companies and unlisted companies. **Not profiled:** extra-budgetary units (municipalities are the example) and companies incorporated outside South Africa. |
| 6–7 | Reasons: collect and classify the activities of all companies in an EG; monitor structural change and demographic events (mergers, takeovers, split-offs); manage relationships and contacts; compensate for administrative sources (tax, trade and industry, labour) that lack full structural information; support economic-survey collection; avoid duplication, over- and under-estimation; help create observation units. |
| 8–9 | Four steps: (1) desktop profiling, (2) data collection, (3) update the SBR, (4) dissemination of the profile. A profile spreadsheet lists subsidiaries, branches and the variables needed to update the register. Every EG is re-profiled after a year; major structural change triggers immediate re-profiling. |
| 10 | No samples are drawn at EG level currently, and no activity classification is made for the group as a whole. Each company in an EG is classified by its own activity as a separate legal unit, so members can carry different SIC codes. The SIC version is not stated. |
| 11 | Duration depends on the number of subsidiaries, branches and divisions, whether accounting records are centralised, and respondent cooperation. Stated ranges: less complex 3–6 months; medium 7 months to 1 year; very complex over 1 year. |
| 12 | Selection: complex structure (holding company, subsidiaries, associates) and EG annual turnover above R100 million. Candidates come from survey-area requests, the "Who Owns Whom in SA" publication or system, and other business publications. The reference year of the threshold is not stated. |
| 13 | Challenges: respondent caution about confidentiality; long completion times; complex activities that generate repeated survey-area queries; constant restructuring; lagging digital technology (AI, machine learning, web scraping). |

## Limitations and open questions

- A presentation, not a manual: no counts, staffing, definitions of "complex", or data on outcomes.
- **Open question (unresolved):** slide 4 shows foreign subsidiaries in an illustrative chart, whereas the [abstract](wbg-stats-sa-profiling-abstract-2025.md) (p. 2) says foreign subsidiaries of an SA holding company are excluded. Possible readings, none confirmed: the chart shows the whole group while only SA entities are loaded; or the chart is illustrative only.
- The "SIC" version, the turnover threshold's reference year, and what "desktop profiling" involves are not stated.
- The slides do not mention kind-of-activity units or geographic units. Whether the SBR holds them for these groups is not documented here.
