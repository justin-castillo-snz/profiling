# Wiesbaden Group: Profiling Processes in Statistics South Africa (abstract)

Howard Shozi (Director, Large Business Unit, Business Register, Statistics South Africa), *Profiling Processes in Statistics South Africa*, abstract for Session 3 (S3.3), "Globalisation and Profiling of Multinational Enterprises", 29th Meeting of the Wiesbaden Group on Business Registers, Johannesburg, 27–31 October 2025.

- **Original:** `WBG - Abstract Session 3 S3.3_StatsSA.pdf` (2 pages). Obtained from the [Wiesbaden Group CIRCABC library](https://circabc.europa.eu/ui/group/ea13cb47-2205-497e-94db-e812a440b486/library/4ac5d0df-0e83-414f-bfb2-35bb7ac5a0ec).
- **Reading copy:** Markdown conversion, not published with this wiki.
- **Companion source:** [the presentation](wbg-stats-sa-profiling-presentation-2025.md), which holds most of the operational detail.
- **Unit:** enterprise group (EG), as profiled by one NSO. Reference period and exact publication date are not given beyond the meeting.

## Evidence guide

| Part | What the abstract says |
|---|---|
| p. 1, introduction | EG profiling is the identification, classification and description of relationships and characteristics of businesses under common ownership or control. The abstract says it outlines methodologies, challenges and lessons learned, aiming to capture ownership structures and intercompany transactions. |
| p. 1, challenges | Heterogeneous enterprise structures hinder standard criteria; ownership and control change dynamically, making profiles hard to keep current; source data in the SBR are inconsistent or incomplete; classification systems may not reflect contemporary practice (for example digital platforms and the gig economy) and lead to misclassification; privacy and confidentiality limit access to detail. |
| p. 2, scope | Stats SA does **not** profile international companies. Ultimate holding companies incorporated abroad are excluded; profiling starts from the SA-incorporated holding company; foreign subsidiaries of an SA ultimate holding company are excluded; companies abroad are not in the SBR. |
| p. 2, closing | Recommends better data-integration frameworks, improved classification systems, adaptive methodologies, and collaboration between statistical agencies and businesses. |

## Limitations

- An abstract: no detailed method, thresholds, counts or quantitative evidence, and some typographical errors. The keyword field contains template prompts, not keywords.
- The challenges are stated generally; the abstract does not say which were observed at Stats SA versus expected in the wider field.
- The exclusion of foreign subsidiaries appears to differ from what the [slides](wbg-stats-sa-profiling-presentation-2025.md) show (see that note's open question).
