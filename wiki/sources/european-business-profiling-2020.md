# Eurostat: European business profiling — Recommendations manual

Eurostat, *European business profiling — Recommendations manual*, 2020 edition. Authors: Vincent Hecquet and Biliana Branska-Lateva. Manuscript completed February 2020; published by the Publications Office of the European Union in 2020. The original PDF (`Eurostat-European business profiling-2020.pdf`) and the Markdown reading copy used for this ingest are not published with this wiki. The conversion is a reading copy, not a second source.

The manual says its statements on policy are informational, not an official European Commission position and not legally binding. It offers recommendations for European collaborative profiling and more general profiling guidance. Its process, terminology and national accounts below describe the situation reported in 2020; they should not be assumed to represent present-day practice or policy.

## Scope and evidence map

The manual focuses on large multinational enterprise groups (GEGs), especially private non-financial groups, and the delineation of global enterprises (GENs) and national enterprises (ENTs). European profiling is a collaborative process involving the NSI in the country of the global decision centre (GDC) and partner NSIs where the group operates. The manual also discusses profiling more generally, including national profiles, business engagement, team capabilities and organizational arrangements.

## Main evidence and qualifications

- The manual treats profiling as analysis of a business's legal, operational and accounting structures to establish statistical units, their links and efficient data-collection arrangements (Foreword, p. 3).
- The European process gives the GDC-country team a global, top-down role and gives partner countries authority to validate and, where needed, reshape their national ENTs. An automatically generated TENT is a proposal, not necessarily the final national enterprise (§§1.C–1.D, pp. 20–26; §§4.A, pp. 67–70).
- The manual describes the IPT as a secure collaborative platform and documents linked group, global-enterprise, temporary-enterprise, national-enterprise and legal-unit attributes (§1.C.1; §§1.D, 4.A, 4.C). It reports 26 NSIs collaborating on 241 GEGs in 2016–2018, with 290 profiles including follow-ups (§5.C.2, p. 88); that passage does not give a country roster and is not evidence of present-day participation.
- For GEN delineation, the manual uses autonomy in decision-making, especially current resource allocation, rather than activity alone. The criteria combine managers and employees, reporting and accounts, responsibility for capital, production resources and economic transactions; segment disclosures and websites can inform, but do not determine, the decision (§§3.A–3.E, pp. 47–64).
- It recommends desk research before contact, direct dialogue where feasible, and coordination with statistical users and partner NSIs. The case for profiling includes more relevant statistical units, reduced double counting and better cross-domain and cross-country consistency (Chapters 1, 3, 5 and 6).
- The organizational models and staffing levels in §6.B and Table 1 are dated examples, not resource standards. The prose and comparison table do not always report identical counts: for example, the French narrative describes 50 manually profiled groups while the table reports 55 and a target of 70; the UK narrative gives 16 profiling FTE while the table lists 13.84; and Dutch prose and table figures differ for Top-X and ConGO populations. Those discrepancies are unresolved in the manual.
- The manual's examples of oil and gas and telecom groups (§3.C.5, pp. 60–61) are sectoral learning, not country-attributed cases. Country authorship of the manual likewise does not establish a country's practice. Country evidence is added only where the text explicitly describes a national model.

## Limitations and use

This is a European 2020 recommendations manual, not Stats NZ guidance. Its method depends on European institutions and tools such as the EuroGroups Register (EGR) and Interactive Profiling Tool (IPT), and its classifications, regulations, calendar, variable requirements and organizational snapshots may have changed. The [Profiling Calendar](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/fpfis/wikis/display/Profiling/Calendar) cited in §4.B redirected to a Webgate sign-in page when checked on 2026-11-26; this observation does not establish current platform access rules. The 2015 operational rules appear as an annex; their inclusion does not establish that each recommendation has the force of law. Verify current local definitions, authority and implementation before applying these approaches elsewhere.
