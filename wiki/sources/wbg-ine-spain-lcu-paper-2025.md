# Wiesbaden Group: The Spanish LCU, structure, procedures and lessons learned (paper and abstract)

Iván Pérez-Plaza and Sixto Muriel (Instituto Nacional de Estadística, INE Spain), *The Spanish Large Case Unit (LCU): structure, procedures, and lessons learned from Intensive Profiling of Multinational Enterprise Groups (MNEs)*. Paper for Session 3 (S3.2), "Globalisation and Profiling of Multinational Enterprises", 29th Wiesbaden Group meeting, Johannesburg, 27–31 October 2025.

- **Original:** `WBG - Abstract Session 3 S3.2_INE_Spain-Abstract-Paper.pdf` (24 pages). Obtained from the [Wiesbaden Group CIRCABC library](https://circabc.europa.eu/ui/group/ea13cb47-2205-497e-94db-e812a440b486/library/4ac5d0df-0e83-414f-bfb2-35bb7ac5a0ec).
- **Reading copy:** Markdown conversion, not published with this wiki.
- **Companion:** [the presentation](wbg-ine-spain-lcu-presentation-2025.md).
- **Date:** the file carries no publication date; its 2025 portfolio figures and roadmap point to 2025.
- **Units:** legal unit (LEU), enterprise (ENT), enterprise group (GEG); control concepts GGH, GDC and UCI. Reference periods: 2022–2025 portfolios (see the labelling caveat below).
- **Layout:** pp. 1–2 abstract (two slightly different versions); pp. 3–21 text; pp. 22–24 bibliography.

## Evidence guide

| Section | What the paper says |
|---|---|
| §2.1 Units | Defines LEU, ENT (smallest combination of legal units, citing Reg. 696/93) and GEG. The Global Group Head (GGH) is the top of the legal hierarchy; the Global Decision Centre (GDC) is where strategic decisions are made regardless of legal position or location; the Ultimate Controlling Institutional Unit (UCI) can be a natural person, public administration, sovereign fund or a resident or non-resident unit. GGH and UCI often but not always coincide; GGH and GDC may or may not. Draws on the Eurostat 2020 manual, the FATS and EGR manuals (2024) and SNA 2025 chapter 6 (pre-edit). |
| §2.2 Modalities | Automatic, manual without contact, and intensive (direct contact, interviews, customised questionnaires) profiling, described as points on a spectrum. LCUs are the most suitable structure for intensive profiling. |
| §3.1 Europe | LCUs began in Sweden (2004), then Ireland, France, Finland and the Netherlands (2009–10); now more than 20 Member States. The voluntary Early Warning System (EWS) dates from 2017. |
| §3.2 Spanish LCU | Created by Ministerial Order ETD/378/2022 of 27 April as a division reporting directly to the INE Presidency. Functions (Art. 2): multiannual intensive profiling plan, direct engagement, coordination, monitoring reorganisations, microdata validation, globalisation analysis, and representation in the LCU network and EWS. It coordinates rather than replaces production units. |
| §4.1 Selection | Two-stage composite complexity index (employees, turnover, countries, subsidiaries). Stage 1 uses the EuroGroups Register (EGR) and global activity for Spanish-controlled MNEs; stage 2 uses the Spanish business register and Spain-only activity whatever the parent's nationality. Top groups are taken within each of ten A10 activity categories. Financial groups are excluded as outside INE business statistics. Portfolio size is limited by resources. |
| §4.2 Portfolio | 5 groups in 2022; 20 in the 2024 portfolio (reference year 2023, all Spanish-controlled); 41 in the 2025 portfolio (reference year 2024, including foreign-headquartered groups). Industry and energy is the largest category. |
| §4.3 Procedure | A formal letter from the INE Presidency to the group's chair or CEO, then an on-site meeting at headquarters. The best contact is the financial consolidation team. Phase 1 fixes the control perimeter, including units missing from registers (branches, special purpose entities, subsidiaries). Phase 2 delineates statistical enterprises and consolidates. Ancillary and integrated units are excluded from the group's total output; production for non-resident units needs separate treatment. Annual calendar to the register and the EGR: group list and GGHs end March t+1; units not in the register end April; legal-unit list mid-September; enterprise units mid-October; EGR update April t+2. Structural business statistics use the previous year's profiling. Follow-up: an annual email asks about perimeter changes; cheaper once the structure is stable. |
| §4.4 Coverage and outcomes | 2024 portfolio: 2.4% of employment, €246bn turnover; 2025 portfolio: 3.2% of employment, €370bn turnover (national GDP given as context only). Spanish-headquartered groups in the 2025 portfolio: 28.5% of employment and 37% of turnover. Marginal coverage per added group declines. Claimed benefits: better delineation, trust, cross-domain coherence (national accounts, fewer revisions) and EU network gains; aggregate gains can hide compensating effects, so value shows at group level. Two anonymised cases are described (§4.4.3); this wiki cites only their qualitative lessons: a group's enterprise count and geographic allocation changed after profiling, and a main-activity reclassification moved a group between sections of the classification. |
| §5 Challenges | Methodological: coherence across the European statistical system, timeliness of structural changes, survey collection poorly suited to the largest groups. Operational: relationship continuity, resourcing (follow-up versus onboarding), multi-source integration and IT, and integration with traditional production lines ("stove pipes"). Lessons: direct contact improves quality; the cross-cutting role gives coherence; European networks (EGR, European profiling, EWS) are essential; groups want centralised reporting. Cites similar centralised collection in Canada, the UK and Ireland. |
| §6 MNEData | A proposed secure digital platform, managed by the LCU, on which a group uploads a predefined variable set for all its resident legal units. Short-term focus: global production arrangements and trade in services. Claimed benefits: consistency, lower burden, a group view, near-real-time data. Risks: commitment, confidentiality and security, technical compatibility, variable dictionary. Indicative timeline: 2025 board approval, 2026 pilot, 2027–28 first operational exercise (reference year 2027). Proposal, not implemented. |
| §7 Conclusions | Intensive profiling and the LCU improve delineation and consistency; European cooperation and centralised reporting are the next steps. |

## Limitations and open questions

- A single-office, self-assessed account that is partly promotional. The benefits (for example fewer revisions) are asserted, with no independent quality measures.
- **Labelling ambiguity (unresolved):** the abstract speaks of 41 groups "by reference year 2024", the chart and table use "2024/2025" portfolio years, and the [presentation](wbg-ine-spain-lcu-presentation-2025.md) labels portfolios by year as 2023 → 20 and 2024 → 41. This wiki uses the paper's "2024 portfolio (reference year 2023)" and "2025 portfolio (reference year 2024)".
- Turnover is turnover generated in Spain, not value added or output; GDP is context only.
- European terminology (NACE, EGR, DIRCE) is specific to the European and Spanish setting, not a universal model.
- Exact values from the anonymised cases are deliberately not reproduced here.
- Bibliography items that are not in the wiki, which are possible future ingests: Muriel, Pérez-Plaza and Cervigón (2024, INE working paper); Hussain, Peltola and Mahajan (2018, UNECE); Ahlborn et al. (2021); Ambroselli et al. (2019); Connolly (2011); Statistics Canada (2018); ONS (2019).
