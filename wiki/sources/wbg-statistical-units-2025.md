# Wiesbaden Group: Statistical Units (2025)

Andrew Allen, *Statistical Units*, presentation for the Wiesbaden Group, October 2025. The original PDF (`WBG - Statistical Units.pdf`) and the Markdown reading copy are not published with this wiki. Slide numbers below refer to the order in the 19-slide deck. The slides give no author affiliation, separate version or statistical reference period.

The presentation brings questions from the UN Committee of Experts on Business and Trade Statistics (UNCEBTS) to the Wiesbaden Group. It discusses which statistical units best measure modern production and how to classify complex multinationals. It is a discussion deck, not an adopted statistical standard or a profiling manual.

## Evidence guide

| Slides | What the presentation says |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Introduces the review of statistical-unit definitions, distinguishes statistical units from legal and administrative units, and asks whether the establishment still works for modern production and how to assign a main activity to complex multinational cases. |
| 5–6 | The joint BPM7 and 2025 SNA research agenda asks whether recommendations on units, including institutional units, should change. Supply and use tables, regional accounts and productivity are among the uses to consider. Allen contrasts enterprise autonomy with establishment detail by activity and location, and notes that the SNA 2008, ISIC Rev. 4 and EU enterprise definitions differed slightly. |
| 7–9 | Production, intermediate consumption, investment and employment are needed with industrial detail, but some businesses cannot supply them all by establishment. The slides cite enterprise-based surveys in much of the EU and the [UK](../countries/united-kingdom.md), broad establishment use in North America and continued legal-unit use in many countries. An enterprise and establishment can coincide in a small business; identifying enterprises in more complex businesses can require profiling judgement. Product detail may suit the establishment; employment records and intellectual property may be held centrally. |
| 10–11 | Establishments can give finer industry detail but may not suit financial information. The deck reports that the EU found comparability issues between enterprises formed by automatic and intensive profiling and was working to improve comparability. It gives no country breakdown, effect sizes or ranking of methods. See [profile quality](../concepts/profile-change-and-quality.md). |
| 12–14 | Legal-unit administrative inputs may need translation to enterprise statistics. The discussion suggests producing statistics on both enterprise and establishment bases, citing the [Netherlands](../countries/netherlands.md) for establishment-based industry statistics. It also mentions the EU's use of kind-of-activity units (KAUs) in short-term statistics. Allen reports that the World Bank uses establishment-based census collection followed by enterprise-level compilation in work with developing countries, but names no country or linkage rule. |
| 15–16 | The ISIC research agenda calls for practical guidance on assigning codes to enterprises, especially in multinational and large-case work. A unit receives one principal code at each level of ISIC. A top-down classification gives that unit a principal activity; agreed finer enterprise profiles and secondary activity information can retain detail. The deck asks what an overall multinational code is actually used for. |
| 17–19 | Cross-border production and economic ownership can affect both unit choice and industry classification. Allen illustrates the classification issue with a London office that owns raw materials and is classified as manufacturing. The presenter asks where definitions, advice on unit choice and implementation guidance might improve, and plans to report the feedback to the UN. |

## What this adds to the wiki

**Interpretation:** three decisions should be recorded separately: how the enterprise is [delineated](../units/group-and-enterprise.md), which unit is [observed or compiled](../units/observation-and-reporting.md), and how the resulting unit is [assigned an activity](../units/activity-and-location.md#activity-classification-after-delineation). Using establishment inputs or a top-down ISIC classification does not establish how the enterprises were formed. The deck therefore adds evidence and open questions to the [approaches catalogue](../analyses/profiling-approaches.md), not a new general approach.

**Limits:** the references to regional and country practice are examples, not a survey by country or statistical product. The Dutch and UK mentions do not describe changes to their profiling methods. The economic-ownership example is illustrative, not UK policy. The presentation does not resolve which outputs should use which units, how to translate between them or whether later guidance changed.
